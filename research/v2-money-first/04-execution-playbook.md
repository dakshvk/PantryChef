## THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE IN THE ENTIRE PROGRAM

**Azoulay, Jones, Kim & Miranda — NBER w24489 / AER: Insights.** Peer-reviewed, 2.7 million US
founders (2007–2014):

- **Mean founder age at founding: 41.9.** For the top 1-in-1,000 highest-growth ventures: **45.0**
- **Under 1% of high-performance startups were founded by 20-year-olds**
- A 50-year-old founder is **1.8x more likely** than a 30-year-old to achieve extreme growth
- Average founding age for true enterprise software: **38**
- **Entrepreneurs were 125% more successful if previously employed in the sector they build in**

**That last number is the one that should change behaviour.** And it shows up in every named success:
Bannerbear's founder was a career product designer. Auxpanel's founder was *a dentist* selling to
dentists. ConvertKit's Nathan Barry was a working blogger selling to bloggers.

> **A statistics undergraduate picking a B2B vertical off a list starts with the single biggest
> documented handicap — and it is the only one on the failure list that can be deliberately bought,
> in under six months, through service work and field research.**

## First-customer channels, ranked by evidence quality

### Tier 1 — do these

**Manual outreach at tiny volume.** The one concrete solo-founder datapoint: a founder **sent 43 cold
emails, got 17 replies, converted 1 paying customer.** Note the shape — *tiny list, high reply, one
customer.* Corroborated structurally: **campaigns under 50 recipients average 5.8% reply vs 2.1% for
large sends.**

Aggregate 2026 benchmarks, all converging: average reply **3.1–3.43%**; top performers 8–12%.
**The secular decline is the important number: 8.5% (2019) → 5% (2025) → 3.43% (2026).** And
**SaaS/software is the worst-performing vertical, often under 2%.**

*Do the arithmetic honestly:* at a good 5% reply and a generous 20% reply→customer rate, **200
hand-written emails produces 10 replies and 2 customers.** At $50/mo that's $100 MRR for two weeks of
work. That is the actual unit of progress.

**Cold calling — the channel where being broke costs you least.** SMB managers connect at
**18–25%** (vs ~16.6% overall, and far better than C-suite). Dial-to-meeting 2–3%; **25–35 dials per
booked meeting.** SMB reps run 80–100 dials/day.

> **~100 dials/day ≈ 3 meetings/day, at zero marginal cost.** No other channel has that cost
> structure. The reason nobody recommends it is that it's miserable, not that it doesn't work.

**Concierge MVP.** The operational rule: **"only automate tasks that customers already paid you to
execute manually."** Critically — customers are *told* the process is hand-operated and pay anyway.
You don't need to pretend there's software.

### Tier 2 — works, slowly or conditionally

**Reddit.** The 10% rule (no more than 1 in 10 actions self-promotional). r/Entrepreneur removes
posts with links; **r/startups prohibits self-promotion outright.** New accounts pitching products
are auto-flagged; shadowbans come without warning. **And the strategic error to avoid:** r/SaaS,
r/startups, r/indiehackers are populated by *other builders*, not buyers.

**SEO.** Measurable traffic months 3–6; meaningful pipeline months 9–12; **commercial-intent keywords
generating demos on a new domain: 12–24 months.** Not a first-customer channel. A month-12+ channel.

**LinkedIn.** Hard cap of **100 connection requests/week** = ~400 touches/month regardless of effort.
Cold-list outreach is dying (10–20% acceptance); intent-triggered is 40–60%. A supplement, not an engine.

### Tier 3 — mostly doesn't work here

**Building in public.** No credible evidence of Twitter BIP converting to B2B revenue from zero
followers. Even the pro-BIP anecdote concedes that a founder with 50,000 followers found **"almost
all actual revenue came from cold emails, conferences, and referrals."**
**Verdict: a credibility artifact and a distribution lottery ticket, not an acquisition channel — and
the highest-status, lowest-ROI use of scarce hours.**

**App marketplaces — the single most useful number found:** of ~700 Shopify apps launched in one
quarter, total revenue across all of them was **$29,471 — mean $39.72 per app, median $0.**
**The median new Shopify app earns zero.** A marketplace listing fulfils demand you already created.

**Trade shows — don't exhibit, do attend.** All-in cost for a 10x10 booth: **$9,000–$45,000.**
But **a $200–600 attendee badge puts 200 of your exact buyers in one hotel for two days** — the
highest-density customer discovery available to someone with no network, and the one place where
*"I'm a UCSB student researching how you handle X"* beats a vendor pitch. **Budget one before writing
any code.**

## The service-first path — best-evidenced route for this founder

**Why it wins:** you are money-poor *and* credibility-poor. Service work inverts both — it pays in
weeks, and every engagement becomes the past performance you lack. It also front-loads the customer
conversations that founders skip.

**Documented transitions:** **Wildbit** — founded 1999 as a web dev consultancy, first web app 2005 →
Postmark/Beanstalk, 100,000+ companies. *Note the honest detail: the founder had to borrow money from
family to cover payroll during the switch.* **Basecamp/37signals** — agency that kept rebuilding the
same internal PM tool for clients, then packaged it. **Pointerpro** — 15 years consulting → **$287K
MRR**. **Bynder** — agency → ~$600M.

**What you can charge in 2026:**
| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Upwork, no history | **$50–85/hr** (US freelance AI dev spans $50–300; median ~$100 mid-level) |
| Direct, after 3–4 engagements | **$100–150/hr** |
| LLM specialisation premium | **+30–50%** |
| Single n8n-style workflow | **$400–1,200** one-time |
| Multi-workflow with AI | **$1,500–4,500** |
| Standard agency shape | **$2,500–15,000 setup + $1,500–5,000/mo retainer** |

**Four $1,500 builds in a quarter = $6,000** — more than most micro-SaaS outcomes achieve in year one.

**The transition mechanism:**
1. Sell the manual outcome first. Tell them it's manual.
2. **Take engagements only in one vertical** — it only works if clients 3, 4 and 5 need what client 1
   needed.
3. Automate only what someone already paid you to do by hand.
4. **The tell you've found the product:** the same deliverable, requested by three unrelated clients,
   that you now produce in 20% of the original time. **That delta is the software.**
5. Expect the crossover to hurt.

⚠️ **Named risk:** service work is a comfortable local maximum. $6K/mo of client work always feels
more urgent than a speculative product. **Set the trigger in advance: "at engagement #5 in the same
vertical, I cap new clients."**

## Pricing — and the ceiling that defines your market

**Benchmarks:** all-B2B median ARPU ~$250/mo · **small-business SaaS $50–200/mo** · self-serve
$20–50/mo. Canonical micro-SaaS ladder: **$29 / $59 / $99** — one documented case moved ARPU from
$31 → $54 and dropped churn below 7%.

**Why $19/mo B2B is a trap:** Stripe's flat **$0.30** makes a $5 charge cost 8.8%. And **SMB churn is
3–7% monthly = 31–58% annually** — structurally, because roughly **one in five new employer
establishments exits within its first year. Your customers die.** At $29/mo with 5% churn, LTV is
~$580; every support hour destroys value. At $99–199 the same hour is defensible.

### The procurement ceiling — the most decision-relevant finding

**SOC 2 becomes effectively mandatory above roughly $50K ACV**, and procurement at 200+ employee
companies routinely blocks vendors without it.

**SOC 2 costs $45,000–70,000 in year one.** Vanta observed median $20,000/yr; Drata $24,869; audit
alone $10,000–50,000.

> **Your addressable band is $29–499/mo, sold to businesses under ~200 employees, where the buyer is
> the budget holder.** Above that: security review, vendor onboarding, legal — with a $45K compliance
> bill and no company to put it on. **Design the target market around this from day one.**

## Real monthly burn

**Bare minimum, pre-revenue:** CA franchise tax $67 (amortised) + Google Workspace $7 + hosting $6 +
domains $3 + LLM $15 + virtual mailbox $12 = **~$110/month**, plus $70 one-time for the LLC.

**Realistic at ~10 customers with cold email running and insured:** **~$450–550/month.**

**Notes:**
- **CA franchise tax is $800/yr minimum** and sources genuinely conflict on the first-year waiver.
  **Confirm with the FTB directly — it's an $800 question.** (SB 122, June 2026, sets a reduced $400
  first-year tax for LLCs formed 2027–2029.)
- **Don't incorporate in Delaware** — operating in California still owes the $800 plus a second
  state's paperwork.
- **E&O insurance from $22.50/mo** (Hiscox). Not legally required — it becomes mandatory *contractually*
  the first time a client's MSA demands proof of insurance. Budget it as a sales-unblocking expense.
- **A virtual mailing address is required by CAN-SPAM** if you cold email. Not your dorm.

**LLM cost model** — 50 customers × 200 requests/mo × (4k in + 1k out):
| Model | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Sonnet 5 (post-1 Sept rates) | **$270** |
| Haiku 4.5 | **$90** |
| Haiku + 90% prompt caching | **~$13–25** |

**Two rules:** route to Haiku by default, escalate on failure — the 5x delta is the difference between
viable and not at $29/mo. **And cache your system prompt** — at 10% of input price it's the biggest
lever available. ⚠️ **Sonnet 5's introductory pricing ends 1 Sept 2026 — four days away** — and would
raise the example above by 50%.

**Legal:** CAN-SPAM permits US B2B cold email without consent but requires a valid physical postal
address and a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days. **FTC penalty up to $53,088 per
email.** California adds Bus. & Prof. Code § 17529.5, commonly cited at **$1,000 per email** — and
you're a California resident.

## Time to revenue — the honest distribution

| Milestone | Median |
|---|---|
| First dollar | 3 months |
| $1K MRR | **12–18 months** |
| $10K MRR | 18–36 months |

**The distribution matters more than the median:**
- **30% never reach $1K MRR and abandon**
- **50% plateau at $1K–$10K**
- 15% reach $10K–$100K · 5% exceed $100K

**80% of attempts end under $10K MRR. The modal outcome of starting a micro-SaaS is a project
abandoned below $1,000/month.** MicroConf's survey of 836 founders found companies under $1,000 MRR
were the single largest group. MicroConf also found **3-founder teams grow 2–3x faster than solo.**

**The most honest sentence found anywhere in this research**, from the Senja.io founders who reached
$5,000 MRR: *"In the early days we tried nearly every growth idea — Reddit posts, Indie Hackers
updates, Slack communities, startup directories, newsletters, cold outreach. **Most brought small
wins but no repeatable traction.**"* **Budget for a long stretch where nothing compounds.**

## How solo founders fail

**CB Insights, 483 post-mortems:** **"no market need" is #1. Nine of the top 20 reasons relate to
customers** — not meeting their needs, not listening, or ignoring them.

1. **Building before selling.** The failure mode a technically strong solo founder is *most* exposed
   to, because building is what you're good at and selling isn't. Every hour coding feels like
   progress and produces no information. **The concierge discipline makes it structurally impossible.**
2. **Choosing a buyer with no budget or authority.** *"Most professors, even enthusiastic champions,
   lack the internal power to get money spent."* **The test: can the person you're talking to put it
   on a credit card without asking anyone?**
3. **Picking a market whose procurement you can't survive.** Founders discover SOC 2 and vendor
   onboarding at the *end* of a six-month cycle, after the champion says yes and security says no.
4. **Burnout before revenue.** 54% of solo founders reported burnout in the past year; 90% of founders
   have considered quitting; 30% abandon before $1K MRR. **There is no external forcing function — no
   investor, no co-founder, no payroll.** In a 12–18 month zero-feedback period, "quietly stop" is the
   path of least resistance. *(This is exactly why the NVC's eight-month cohort structure may be worth
   more than its prize money.)*
5. **Domain outsiderism.** See the NBER finding above.

## What the evidence actually recommends

1. **Do not start with a product.** Modal outcome is abandonment below $1K MRR; #1 documented failure
   cause is building before selling.
2. **Start with paid service work in one vertical.** Pays in weeks, buys the sector experience worth
   +125%, and is how Wildbit, Basecamp, Pointerpro and Bynder all started.
3. **Buy domain standing deliberately.** One trade conference badge. Fifty customer conversations.
   Use "UCSB statistics student researching X" as the door-opener — it works *because* it isn't a pitch.
4. **First customers come from ~100 cold calls/day and 20–50 hand-written emails** — not Twitter,
   Reddit, SEO, or an app store. Remember the Shopify median of $0 and the 43-emails-to-1-customer datapoint.
5. **Constrain to sub-200-employee businesses at $49–499/mo where the buyer holds the budget.**
6. **NVC info sessions, October — five weeks out.** 1517 Medici Grant this week. URCA around 1 Oct.
7. **Keep burn at ~$110/month.** Haiku + prompt caching. Own books. No CRM. Confirm the $800 franchise
   tax question with the FTB.
8. **Automate only what someone has paid you to do by hand three times.**
