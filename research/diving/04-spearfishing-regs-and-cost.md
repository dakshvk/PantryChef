# PART 1 — THE SPEARFISHING REGULATION IDEA

## ⚠️ I have to reverse my own recommendation

Last round I ranked this **#1, best fit for you**. Researched properly, **it doesn't survive.** The
product already exists, it's free, and there are four of them.

## What already ships

| Product | Coverage | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Fish Rules** | Recreational saltwater regs for **state AND federal waters, Maine to Texas, California, Hawaii and the Caribbean**, plus freshwater for FL, AL, TX. Uses GPS and the calendar to show only rules in effect right now. **Works offline.** Species images for ID. Separate commercial version. | **Free** (optional Pro Staff tier for expert photo ID) |
| **FishLegal** | All California MPA maps with boundaries + all CA saltwater regs, GPS positioning relative to MPAs, **full offline capability**, continuous updates | Free |
| **CDFW Ocean Sportfishing Interactive Web Map** | Official California state map | Free |
| **ProtectedSeas** | MPA database with area-specific regulations across **25+ different activities** | Free |

**The exact product I described — "can I legally take this fish, at this size, here, today?" — is Fish
Rules, and it has been shipping for years across the entire US coastline.**

## The complexity is real — it's just already handled

For the record, because it's worth knowing how hard this domain is:

- **Species × method.** In Florida state waters **Permit and African Pompano are hook-and-line only —
  but spearing is legal for them in federal waters.** Same species, same day, same gear, different
  side of a line you cannot see.
- **Method bans by state.** **Hawaii prohibits scuba for spearfishing** entirely, for fair-chase
  reasons. California permits both scuba and breath-hold. Florida bans harvest by rebreather for
  everything **except lionfish**.
- **Species carve-outs.** California prohibits spearing giant (black) sea bass, garibaldi, gulf
  grouper, broomtail grouper, trout, salmon and broadbill.
- **Geography.** MPAs, the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary, and a specific prohibition on
  spearfishing in the Upper Keys from Long Key to Key Largo.
- **Even transit is regulated.** You may cross an MPA that bans spearfishing with catch aboard only if
  your gear is unloaded and not in hand, and you stay at the surface — no stopping, anchoring or
  entering the water.

## And the penalties are genuinely serious

Unlike the restaurant food-waste mandates, this has real teeth:

- **Florida: $50–$500 per fish** for a first offence on undersized fish; multiple fish or protected
  species can be a misdemeanour with **up to 60 days in jail**. Spearfishing violations are a **Level
  Two violation** under Florida law.
- **California: fines exceeding $1,000**, misdemeanour charges, potential jail.
- A documented federal case: **two years' probation and a $2,500 criminal fine** for illegal
  spearfishing in Florida.

**So the pain is real and the forcing function is real. The problem is that it's already solved, for
free, four times over.**

## The one residual gap — and why it's still not a company

⚠️ **I could not verify whether Fish Rules filters by gear method.** If it shows hook-and-line rules
without distinguishing spearfishing restrictions, there is a narrow wedge — the species × method ×
jurisdiction matrix above is genuinely distinct for spearos.

**But that is a feature request to Fish Rules, not a startup.** They have the data pipeline, the
coastline coverage, the offline app and the users. Adding a gear-type filter is a sprint for them and
a company for you.

### Verdict: ❌ Don't build it.

**What to keep from it:** the method was right even though the answer was wrong. *Check whether the
thing already exists before ranking it #1* — which is exactly the mistake I made, and exactly the
mistake the diver wishlist made.

# PART 2 — WHY DIVING COSTS WHAT IT DOES

**The important finding: the price is engineered, not incidental.** It is held up by four
interlocking mechanisms, all deliberate.

## Mechanism 1 — Enforced retail pricing

- Manufacturers set **MSRP** and a **MAP** (minimum advertised price) beneath it.
- **Major brands do not allow authorised dealers to sell online or ship products at all.** Scubapro is
  named specifically.
- Dealers can discount roughly **10–15%**, and no further.
- **Dealers have been threatened with losing their dealership for giving customers larger discounts
  than the manufacturer allowed.**

**The stated purpose is explicit:** territories and dealer margins are *protected* to maintain loyalty
to manufacturers and to keep local dive shops viable enough to employ knowledgeable staff.

## Mechanism 2 — Parts lockdown

> **Aqualung, Atomic, Cressi, Scubapro and others do not sell regulator service kits or parts to
> consumers at all**, arguing that consumers do not have a right to repair, on liability and safety
> grounds.

Their argument is not frivolous — *"a badly serviced regulator or a low-quality o-ring can be
catastrophic at depth."* It is life-support equipment. But the effect is that **the only legal path to
a serviced regulator runs through an authorised dealer.**

## Mechanism 3 — Warranty coupled to recurring authorised service

- Scubapro recommends service **every 24 months or 100 dives**, by a certified technician at an
  authorised dealer.
- **Most manufacturers require annual authorised service to maintain the warranty and the "free parts
  for life" programme.**

**"Free parts for life" is the elegant part of the design: the parts are free, and you pay labour
every year, forever, at a shop the manufacturer controls.** Stop, and you lose both.

## Mechanism 4 — The used market is booby-trapped

- **Buying secondhand voids the warranty** — and therefore the free-parts programme.
- **Service kits for a model may be discontinued**, or the brand no longer supported. *"Very old
  models you can't service because you can't get spare parts are common in the used market."*
- A used regulator **must be overhauled before first use**: **$100–300+**.

**So the obvious escape from a $1,000–3,000 purchase leads to a device that may be unserviceable, and
costs $100–300 before you can breathe off it.**

## The stack, end to end

| Line | Cost |
|---|---|
| Certification | $350–900 |
| Mask, snorkel, fins — **before the first pool session** | $300–600 |
| Full personal gear | **$1,000–3,000** |
| Annual servicing | $100–200 *(5–8% of gear value/yr)* |
| Boat dive, two tanks | $75–150 |
| Shore dive | **$10–20 air fill** |
| **20 dives a year, incl. one modest trip** | **$1,500–2,500/yr** |

## THE TENSION NOBODY IN THE INDUSTRY WANTS TO SAY OUT LOUD

> **The price floor exists to keep dive shops alive. Dive shops are how the industry creates divers.
> And the cost is a named cause of the dropout that is shrinking the sport.**

The structure protecting the channel is helping to drain the pool the channel fishes in. **Every
cost-reduction play in scuba is therefore also an attack on the training channel** — which is why
DGX's founder *"took flak for supposedly destroying the dive industry by selling dive equipment on the
internet."*

That is a genuine strategic tension, not a rhetorical one. Take it seriously before picking a side.

## SO HOW DO YOU MAKE IT CHEAPER? — ranked, with what each needs

### 0. ⭐ The market already answered: it's called freediving

| | Freediving | Scuba |
|---|---|---|
| Full kit | **$300–600** *(to ~$950)* | **$1,250–3,000+** |
| Tanks and fills | **None** | Recurring |
| Mandatory annual authorised servicing | **None** | Yes |
| Shore access | **Free or near-free** | $10–20 fill minimum |
| Course length | Shorter | Longer |

**And the participation data matches exactly: freediving +52% since 2021, US scuba certifications
−35% since 2001.**

> **People are not waiting for someone to make scuba cheaper. They are leaving scuba for the thing
> that already is.** Any cost play inside scuba swims against both a defended price structure *and* a
> migration that has already started.

### 1. The serviceability database — cheapest to build, real gap

**The problem, straight from the research:** a buyer of a used regulator cannot tell whether it is a
bargain or a brick. Are service kits still made? Is the brand still supported? Which independent
technicians service it? What does an overhaul actually cost?

**Why this one is interesting:** it doesn't fight the price structure directly — it **makes the used
market work**, and the used market is the single biggest available lever on cost.

- **What's needed:** model-by-model parts and support data (partly public via manuals, partly hostile
  to collect), a directory of independent technicians, and the discipline to keep it current.
  **Cost: time, not capital.** No safety-critical failure mode if framed as reference, not advice.
- ⚠️ **The honest problem: it monetises badly.** Affiliate revenue on used gear sales is small, and
  the people who most need it are the ones least willing to pay.

### 2. Direct-to-consumer gear — proven, but not a student project

**DGX Gears is the working proof.** Own-brand regulators, **published service manuals, and all service
kits and parts sold without restriction** — explicit right-to-repair.

And the industry's own analysts say the door is open:

> *"D2C in the dive industry could be more easily implemented by a startup without an already
> established network of resellers, similar to how Dell disrupted the PC industry"* — because a
> startup has no dealer relationships to protect.

- **What's needed:** capital, an OEM manufacturing relationship, **product liability insurance on
  life-support equipment**, inventory, and a tolerance for being hated by every dive shop in your
  town. **This is a funded hardware company.**

### 3. Shore-diving infrastructure — the only one you could start this weekend

**A shore dive costs a $10–20 air fill. A boat dive costs $75–150.** The cheap path already exists;
it's just invisible. Entries, parking, surface conditions, hazards, tides, what's actually down there,
and whether you can do it without a buddy who owns a boat.

- **What's needed:** legwork and local knowledge. Nothing else.
- ⚠️ **But** it overlaps the visibility-forecast wave (DiveSight, DiveViz, Marla Blue, VizFinder,
  Viz-app) and the buddy-matching wave (Dive Buddy, Buddy Social) — both crowded, both recent, and
  neither obviously making money.

### 4. What *won't* work

- **A price-comparison site.** MAP pricing means the prices are the same everywhere by design. There
  is nothing to compare.
- **A group-buying scheme.** Dealers lose their dealership for discounting beyond the allowance.
- **Anything that assumes gear is expensive because of manufacturing cost.** It isn't. It's expensive
  because the channel is protected.

## THE ONE-LINE VERSION

**Spearfishing regs:** already free, four times over — don't build it.

**Cost:** diving is expensive on purpose, to fund the shops that create divers; the cheap alternative
already exists and is growing 52%; and of the three ways to attack the price, the only one you could
start alone is a serviceability database that makes the used market work — **which is genuinely useful
and probably doesn't make money.**
