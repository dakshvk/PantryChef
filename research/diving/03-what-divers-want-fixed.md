## THE SETUP: THE WISHLIST WAS STALE

The community wishlist — the features divers say on forums and in videos that they wish existed —
checks out badly against what is purchasable in 2026.

| The wish | Status | What actually exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Underwater GPS & live navigation** | 🟢 **OPEN** | **The only genuinely unsolved item.** Radio doesn't penetrate water. Garmin DiveView gives bathymetry for 4,000+ sites, but it is a *surface planning* feature — underwater, anything positional is dead reckoning with unbounded drift. |
| Underwater diver-to-diver texting | ❌ SHIPPED | **Garmin SubWave** — preset messages at 30m, 8-diver network, buddy tank pressure within 10m |
| Smart HUD in the mask | ❌ SHIPPED ×3 | **Shearwater NERD 2 ($1,650)**, **Scubapro Galileo HUD**, **Halcyon Symbios HUD**. Adoption problem, not invention problem: price, and **fogging or a droplet obscures a near-eye display** in a way it never obscures a wrist screen. Mostly technical and rebreather divers. |
| Biometric adjustment for workload and cold | ❌ SHIPPED | **Scubapro ZH-L16 ADT MB PMG** already folds **heart rate, skin temperature and breathing rate** into workload and adapts the deco plan — Galileo Sol, G2, G3. Scubapro claims it's the only algorithm using respiration rate as a workload indicator. |
| Built-in gas analyzer | ⚠️ PARTIAL | **Apeks DSX** ships with an oxygen-analyzer accessory; **Divesoft DNA** is a pocket nitrox analyzer that pairs to a phone over Bluetooth. Market has chosen modularity. |
| Universal air-integration standard | ⚠️ PARTIAL | Already exists across four brands — Oceanic, Aqualung, Sherwood, Shearwater all use Pelagic transmitters (`FCC ID MH8A`). **Garmin and Suunto hold out on purpose.** |

> ⭐ **Five of six shipped or half-shipped, and the one that didn't is blocked by physics.**
> A wishlist scraped from forums is a list of things the market has usually already answered.
> Community chatter lags product by years — a diver complaining about mask HUDs may simply not know
> the NERD 2 has existed for a decade, or may be complaining about **its price**, which is an
> entirely different problem.

**Both "partials" are business problems in technical costume.** The gas analyzer stays separate
because an O₂ cell is a consumable that drifts and needs calibration — a poor thing to seal inside a
pressure accessory. The transmitter standard stays fragmented because **the accessory is the
lock-in**. Neither is fixed by a better idea; both would be fixed by a manufacturer giving something
up.

## SO WHAT DO DIVERS ACTUALLY WANT FIXED?

**Not features. Friction.** And the industry has documented its own problem in detail.

### The number that reframes everything

> **In the US, more scuba divers drop out each year than are certified** — despite 100,000+ new
> certifications annually. Participation is net negative and has been for years.

That is the same finding as the certification decline from the first research file (198,000 in 2001 →
128,000 in 2023), seen from the other end.

### The dropout curve

| Interval | Dropout |
|---|---|
| One year after entry-level certification | **~15%** *(DEMA, 1988 — old, but the only longitudinal figure found)* |
| Four years | **>50%** |
| Among everyone who has tried scuba at least once | **96.3%** ⚠️ *stated without methodology; treat as directional* |

### The five named causes — from the industry's own literature

**1. The confidence gap.** *"Training that does not adequately qualify them to get out there and
enjoy diving on a regular basis. At the core of this problem is confidence, or more to the point, its
absence in many newly certified divers."* People finish the course certified but not competent enough
to organise a dive alone.

**2. Lifestyle friction — the strongest of the five.** *"Scuba cannot be done on a whim after work.
Equipment, travel, refresher training, boat fees. None are unreasonable alone, but together they form
a recurring threshold. Divers who do not integrate diving into a predictable routine often perceive
each return as 'starting over,' financially and mentally."*

**3. No shown progression.** *"Once certified, they weren't shown where or how to dive next.
Certification was treated like a finish line instead of a starting line."*

**4. Low customer satisfaction with operators** — named directly as a driver of dropout. The
qualitative complaints found: rental gear running out, defective kit (broken O-rings) with unhelpful
staff and no tools on hand, hidden fees, opaque and inconsistent pricing between customers on the
same boat, crowded classes with too little instructor attention, being rushed, and condescending or
sexist treatment.

**5. Skills decay faster than most people dive.** Skills degrade measurably after **as little as six
months** of inactivity, and **PADI recommends a refresher for anyone who hasn't dived in six
months.** A refresher is roughly **two hours of knowledge review and up to six hours total**, plus
pool time with an instructor.

> **Combine that with "fewer than 42% of certified divers dive even once a year" and the trap is
> visible: the majority of certified divers are permanently in refresher-owing status.** Every return
> costs money, a day, and an admission that you're rusty. That is the "starting over" feeling,
> precisely.

## THE COST STACK — why "recurring threshold" is the right phrase

| Line | Cost |
|---|---|
| Entry-level certification | **$350–900** *(to slightly over $1,000 with logistics)* |
| Personal mask, snorkel, fins — **required before the first pool session** | **$300–600** |
| A full personal gear set | **$1,000–3,000** |
| Annual gear servicing | **$100–200** *(rule of thumb: 5–8% of gear value per year)* |
| Boat dive, two tanks | **$75–150 per trip** |
| Shore dive | **$10–20 air fill** — the one genuinely cheap option |
| Full rental set | $25–50/day |
| **Active diver, 20 dives a year, incl. one modest trip** | **$1,500–2,500/yr** |

**Note the $300–600 that lands before the first pool session** — it is charged before a student knows
whether they even like breathing underwater.

## WHAT'S ALREADY BEEN BUILT AGAINST THIS

**Buddy matching is taken.** **Dive Buddy** (verified-ID buddy search, zero-fee dive-centre booking,
site conditions) and **Buddy: Social Dive App** (Buddy Radar matching, **$2.99/month or $29.99/year**;
free dive log and gear management) both ship, plus Dive with Buddy. ⚠️ *No usage or revenue data was
obtainable — and given Deepblu's fate, assume paid conversion is poor until shown otherwise.*

**Refresher is an agency product.** PADI ReActivate, DAN's skills refresher, agency inactive-diver
courses. It is sold as a course, delivered by a shop, and priced as a lesson.

## THE HONEST VERDICT

> **The biggest problem in recreational diving is retention, and it does not have a software answer
> that a solo student can sell.**

Three reasons, stated plainly rather than worked around:

1. **The causes are service problems, not information problems.** Confidence comes from supervised
   water time. A better app does not make someone competent enough to organise a dive alone.
2. **The delivery mechanism is the dive shop**, and dive shops already buy management software at
   $199/month and up (Bloowatch, EVE, Dive Shop 360, Divery, Dive Admin, Diversdesk) with agency
   endorsement as the moat. **Retention tooling — lapsed-diver detection, six-month refresher
   triggers, filling empty boat seats — is a feature for those incumbents, not a company.**
3. **The obvious consumer answers are taken and probably not paying.** Buddy matching ships from at
   least three vendors; social logging killed Deepblu after ten years.

### What is genuinely still open, in order

1. **Underwater positioning.** The one unanswered wish. Blocked by physics, which is why it's still
   open — a hardware and signal-processing problem, not an app.
2. **Higher-bandwidth short-range acoustic comms.** Real headroom between Garmin's 45-second preset
   at 30m and the ~1 Mbps-at-100m the research literature reaches. Also hardware.
3. **Spearfishing regulation compliance** — still the best fit for a statistics undergraduate, for
   the reasons in file 01: public-record data, fragmentation as the moat, no safety-critical failure
   mode.

### The one thing worth taking from this file regardless of what you build

**The wishlist was five-sixths obsolete, and I only found that out by checking each item against what
is purchasable.** Forum chatter, video comments and "top 10 things users want" listicles describe a
market that existed several years ago.

> **This is the strongest argument yet for the fifteen-interview method: ask people what they did
> last time, not what they wish existed.** What people wish for is often already on a shelf they
> haven't looked at, or is priced at $1,650 and they're really complaining about the price.
