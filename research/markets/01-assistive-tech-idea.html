Whoever already owns both owns the feature by default — which is why every incumbent in every segment has it, and why an outsider arriving with only the logic has nothing to attach it to.

Which means the "moat" wasn't one

PantryChef's deterministic safety layer is genuinely good engineering, and I was right that consumer recipe apps don't have it. I was wrong about why. Not because nobody thought of it — because in every market where it matters, it's a feature of the system that already holds the menu and the diner's record. The logic was never the scarce part.

The pattern, stated once

Look at what the winners in this research actually owned. onX: county parcel records nobody would compile. Fishbrain: seven million logged catches. Strava: a social graph. AllTrails: a decade of reviews.

Not one of those was an idea. Each was the residue of someone doing something unglamorous for years, or being inside a thing long enough to see what outsiders couldn't. There is no search query that returns those.

The two survivors have the same property: they cost time rather than cleverness, and that is precisely why they're still standing.

Everything cheap to attempt has been attempted. The filter isn't "is this a good idea" any more — it's "would a normal person give up before finishing this." A diver-worn sonar that needs a year of pool testing, and fifteen conversations that need you to walk into fifteen businesses, both pass. Nothing else in seven attempts did.
