Why this discipline, specifically

The economics are broken in a way that creates room

The perversity: insurance readily covers dedicated speech-generating devices, but tablet AAC apps typically do not qualify for Medicare or Medicaid coverage.
OptionCostInsurance
Dedicated speech-generating device$3,000–15,000✅ Covered
Tablet + AAC app (Proloquo2Go, TouchChat, LAMP)$150–400❌ Usually not

So "make it cheaper with AI" does not work here — and that's the important thing to understand before touching this market. The expensive device survives because reimbursement rewards it, not because it's better. The family isn't price-sensitive; a third party pays. Undercutting on price fights the funding structure and loses.

But look at what the money buys: a device that gets abandoned in 30 to 50 percent of cases.

That is a staggering failure rate for equipment costing thousands of dollars, approved by clinicians, funded by insurance, and given to a child who cannot speak. The problem was never the hardware. The hardware works. Something after the hardware is failing.

The idea

Not a device. The layer that makes the device get used.

A child already has a tablet with an AAC app. It logs every button press. Nobody looks at those logs, and nobody around the child is coordinated.

1

Read the negative space, not the hits

Existing AAC analytics count what a child said. The interesting signal is what they tried to say and couldn't — navigating into a category and backing out, hitting the same near-miss button repeatedly, abandoning a sequence halfway. Those are words the child is reaching for and does not have.

2

Turn that into a vocabulary recommendation

Not generic core-word lists. This child, this week, is reaching for something in the "feelings" branch and giving up. The literature is explicit that functional, personalised, meaningful vocabulary reduces abandonment — and that noun-heavy sets are part of the failure.

3

Push the same five words to every adult

This is the actual product. The SLP models one set, the teacher another, the parent doesn't know what either did — and "carryover across providers" is named as a significant barrier. One shared weekly focus, delivered to all three, with what to model and when.

4

Let the documentation fall out for free

Special educators spend 6–10 hours drafting a single IEP and 3–4 more per annual review. If the system already holds structured evidence of attempts, growth and modelled vocabulary, progress monitoring writes itself — which is the thing that gets a district to pay.

The child's device stays exactly as it is. You're not competing with Proloquo2Go or a $12,000 device — you're making the one they already own stop being abandoned.

That's the same shape as the businesses that actually won in the last research file: onX didn't build a better map, it assembled the parcel data nobody else would. Here the unassembled dataset is what non-speaking children try to say and fail to — and it compounds, because every child using it makes the recommendations better for the next.
The line you cannot cross

The AI never speaks for the child

There is a paper titled "The less I type, the better": How AI Language Models can Enhance or Impede Communication for AAC Users. That title is a warning, and it's from the community itself.

An LLM generating utterances for a non-speaking person is putting words in the mouth of someone who cannot correct you. It is the single most damaging thing this product could do, and it is also the most tempting demo.

So take the same invariant that worked for PantryChef's allergen engine and apply it here: the model may only ever make suggestions to the adults about which vocabulary to make available. It never generates speech, never completes a sentence, never puts a word on the screen that the child did not choose. Enlarging someone's vocabulary is help. Speaking on their behalf is not.

Before anything else

Four things I did not verify, in order

Applying the lesson properly this time: these come before the market sizing and the build plan, not after.

1

Do AAC apps actually expose usage logs?

The whole idea rests on this and I did not check it. Proloquo2Go, TouchChat and LAMP all collect data — but whether a third party can read it, via API or export, is unknown to me. If the answer is no, the idea is dead in its current form. One afternoon of reading developer documentation answers it.

2

Re-run the competitor check, properly

One search returned no startups. That is not the same as none existing. Search the AAC vendor sites themselves, the ASHA exhibitor lists, and the special-ed edtech funding announcements. Five ideas have died on this step; assume this one will too until it doesn't.

3

The regulatory load

Children's data, in schools, about disability. FERPA, COPPA, and possibly HIPAA all plausibly apply, and I have researched none of them. This is a genuine cost, not a formality, and it may be the real barrier that explains the empty market.

4

Talk to five SLPs before writing code

Same instrument as the restaurant questionnaire. Ask what they did with the last child who abandoned a device — not whether they'd like your product. If "carryover" and "vocabulary" don't come up unprompted, the thesis is wrong.

More important than the idea

Why this discipline surfaced something and twenty outdoor markets didn't

Compare the two searches. Outdoor conditions: free weather APIs, a weekend of work, five competitors per activity, zero regulatory load. Assistive communication: a 30–50% failure rate that has persisted for years, a third-party payer, a regulated data environment, and academic research that hasn't been productised.

The friction is the opportunity. Markets that are easy to enter have already been entered. The ones with an ugly barrier — regulation, procurement, specialist knowledge, a dataset nobody wants to assemble — are the ones where a solo founder who is willing to do the unglamorous part can still get in.

The filter that would have saved you five ideas: does this market have something in it that makes normal people give up?

Where the answer is no, five apps already exist. Where the answer is yes — parcel records for onX, seven million logged catches for Fishbrain, children's disability data here — that difficulty is the only thing standing between you and everyone else with the same idea. Run that filter across other disciplines and you'll generate better candidates than another list of twenty.
