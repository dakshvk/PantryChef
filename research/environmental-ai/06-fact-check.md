# Adversarial Fact-Check — Verdicts

_Independent verification pass, Aug 2026. Method: exact-phrase and multi-framing search probes
across distinct source domains, run against the claims — not against the files that made them.
WebFetch was egress-blocked here too, so verdicts rest on independent search corroboration._

## THE HEADLINE: the leading recommendation did not survive

**Two of the six pillars supporting "California wildfire defensible-space software" are false, and
they are pillars one and two.**

### ❌ REFUTED — Zone 0 is not in force

The Board of Forestry voted 8–0 on **19 Aug 2026 to approve the *final draft***. **OAL review has
not happened. There is no statewide effective date.** Nothing is enforceable. A page titled
literally *"Approved Aug 19, 2026 — not yet law"* is the top organic result on the subject.

### ❌ REFUTED — the 1 Jan 2027 / 1 Jan 2028 deadlines do not exist

The approved rule applies immediately to **new construction** on adoption. Existing structures get
**Phase 1 within 3 years** (combustibles, gutters, dead plants, tree trimming) and **Phase 2 within
5 years** (under-eave zone, combustible gates, sheds, fencing — timeline assigned by the local
jurisdiction), both running from an effective date **that does not yet exist**.

> The "twelve months to stand up a workflow" premise is off by roughly a factor of three to five.
> "You arrive as the requirement lands, not five years late" is **precisely inverted** — for
> existing homes the requirement lands in roughly three to five years.

### ❌ REFUTED — SB/DVBE does not reach local fire agencies

The mechanism is real and correctly described, but it is a **California state-agency** procurement
authority (Gov. Code §§14838.5/14838.7). **Cities, counties, special districts and local fire
agencies run their own purchasing codes.** They may honor the SB certification as reciprocity
partners, but the no-bid $249,999.99 pathway does not extend to the recommended buyer.

This was the "procurement structurally favors you" pillar — the one channel where "no past
performance is not disqualifying." It does not favor you with this buyer.

### ⚠️ PARTIAL — the incumbent is stronger than assessed

"100+ agencies across 12 states" is confirmed **only as text on Fire Aside's own site** — a vendor
self-claim, exactly as feared. But real pricing surfaced: **Berkeley's contract may be extended at
not-to-exceed $85,000/year** — far above the inferred "$5k–$50k/yr." A founder anchored on the low
guess would underprice against an incumbent already clearing $85k on one mid-size city.

### ⚠️ Also material

**CAL FIRE's $135M is confirmed** — but the **FY25-26 application window closed 6 Aug 2025**,
grantees are agencies and nonprofits rather than vendors, and **software eligibility is not stated
in any guideline surfaced.**

## ⭐ THE OPPOSITE RESULT: Phase I ESA came through intact and reinforced

**Every claim supporting it verified**, including the D.C. Circuit ruling that *expands* the
liability driving it:

- **CERCLA PFOA/PFOS designation UPHELD 18 Aug 2026** — D.C. Circuit denied the industry petitions,
  applying *Loper Bright* to independently construe CERCLA §102(a). ✅
- **Phase I ESA cost $2,200–$4,000** corroborated; **ASTM E1527-21 is the EPA-recognized All
  Appropriate Inquiries standard** for the CERCLA liability defense. ✅
- **WOTUS: 81% confirmed from the agencies' own analysis** — 17.36M of 90.91M NWI-mapped acres (19%)
  retain jurisdiction. Proposed 20 Nov 2025, not final. ✅

> On verification quality alone, Phase I ESA is now the better-evidenced of the two.

## The four most dangerous errors

1. **The Zone 0 deadline.** A founder who builds a 90-day plan on "every VHFHSZ agency must
   operationalize Zone 0 by 1 Jan 2027" walks into fifteen customer interviews and is told the rule
   isn't in effect and nobody has a date. The urgency pitch — the entire reason it ranked #1 —
   evaporates in the first meeting. **It is the kind of error a fire marshal catches instantly**,
   and it costs credibility with exactly the buyer you cannot afford to lose.
2. **SB/DVBE applied to local agencies.** Weeks spent self-certifying on Cal eProcure expecting it
   to unlock no-bid sales to fire districts — it will not.
3. **Fire Aside pricing.** Pricing a pilot at $5k–$15k against an incumbent clearing $85k/yr at one
   city means underpricing *and* underestimating entrenchment.
4. **The "200 working days" quote.** Presented as the most citable number in Wave 1; appears not to
   exist. Putting an uncorroborated quotation in a deck with a journal attribution is exactly the
   failure mode this pass was built to prevent.

## Verdict on the leading recommendation

> **It does not hold up in its current form.**
>
> What survives: the regulation is real and was approved 19 Aug 2026; California genuinely is the one
> jurisdiction adding environmental mandates while federal ones are dismantled (claims on NEPA, SB 261
> and WOTUS all confirm the contraction thesis); 10 CCR §2644.9 and the FAIR Plan's 12 discounts are a
> real, verified second demand driver; $135M of CAL FIRE money exists; the geography is real; and a
> $350M utility-VM exit confirms the category has value.
>
> What breaks: the urgency is fabricated, the procurement advantage doesn't apply to the named buyer,
> and the competitor is stronger than assessed.
>
> **Demote from "fastest path to first dollar" to a credible but ordinary opportunity with a
> slow-burning regulatory tailwind and a funded incumbent.** If kept at the top, step one of the
> 90-day plan is not self-certifying on Cal eProcure — it is **calling three fire marshals to ask what
> their actual local purchasing threshold is and when they believe Zone 0 becomes enforceable.**
> On current evidence the honest answer to the second question is *nobody knows yet*.
