## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH TAKEAWAY (Funding agent)

> Across 2023–2026, **every environmental-AI category that survived did so by selling to a buyer
> whose payment was compelled by liability, regulation, or capital pricing** — utilities facing
> ignition liability, insurers pricing a book, financial institutions under disclosure rules, EHS
> teams facing fines, navies and offshore developers. Every category that depended on **voluntary
> corporate virtue** — carbon offsets, biodiversity credits, nature markets, conservation
> monitoring — either collapsed or never had commercial revenue in the first place and knows it.
> For a solo founder with no capital and no hardware, the defensible move is not to pick the most
> environmentally important problem; it is to pick the **underserved tier** — small utilities,
> mid-market importers, municipal water systems — inside a category where somebody is already
> legally required to pay.

## Categories with PROVEN paying buyers — ranked

**1. Utility vegetation management & wildfire risk** ⭐ strongest evidence in the report.
Three companies raised $43M–$58.5M within 20 months: **Pano AI $44M** (total $89M, 15 US utilities
incl. APS, PGE, Xcel), **Overstory $43M** (Nov 2025, serves 6 of the 10 largest utilities in the
Americas), **AiDash $58.5M** (total $160.5M, National Grid, Avista). Utilities buy because wildfire
ignition is **existential liability — PG&E-scale, bankruptcy-scale.** Rate-base recoverable, repeats
annually.

**2. EHS / environmental compliance software.** $1.9B → $4.5B at 14.6% CAGR. **EQT paid $3B for
Avetta**; Thoma Bravo explored ~$2B for Cority. Never had a hype cycle, never had a crash.

**3. Insurance-adjacent physical climate risk.** **MSCI paid $120M cash for First Street**
(property-level risk on 2.4B structures). Stand Insurance raised $35M; Faura and Delos funded;
Liberty Mutual and Tokio Marine invested directly in Pano.

**4. Maritime / defense / offshore-energy ocean data.** **Windward exited at ~$280M** to FTV Capital —
the largest clean exit in the report, and its buyers are shipping, trade compliance and sanctions
enforcement, **not conservation.** Saildrone raised $60M + $50M strategic from Lockheed and now
leads with "Maritime Defense." Bedrock's $25M Series A was backed by paid survey work for offshore
wind and O&G.

**5. Datacenter energy — but only when sold as cost.** Emerald AI (NVIDIA, Lowercarbon) sells
*faster interconnection*. Greenpixie sells "FinOps + GreenOps" and leads with cloud spend.

**6. Water utility operations.** Current Software went from a **$2.75M seed to 80 utilities in
16 states.** Unglamorous, slow, pays.

**7. Enterprise carbon accounting — real but shrinking.** EU Omnibus removed most of the future
demand curve; exits are modest ($64M for Plan A mostly in stock, ~$100M for Sustain.Life) against
$19M–$179M raised. **Avoid.**

## Categories that ONLY ever had grant money

**1. Biodiversity / nature credits.** The clearest no-buyer evidence found anywhere: of ~**11.6
million** credits issued or projected, **124,183 sold and 1,285 retired.** The market is
**~20 transactions, usually below $50k.** A survey of 16 nature-credit companies found credits used
mainly for "marketing and branding," most seeing **no direct commercial benefit.**

**2. Conservation technology.** Ai2's **Skylight** — the leading IUU-fishing detection platform — is
described by Ai2 itself as one of several **"zero-revenue AI for the environment programs,"** its API
is explicitly **"not intended for commercial use,"** and it is given to conservationists free.
Global Fishing Watch is free. Wild Me had to merge into another nonprofit.
**This is not a "nobody built it" market. It is a "the best-funded, best-engineered players
deliberately give it away because nobody will pay" market.**

**3. Coral restoration** (Coral Vita had to invent eco-tourism revenue) · **4. Ocean CDR** (Running
Tide dead, SeaO2 bankrupt, Microsoft paused) · **5. Open methane monitoring** (MethaneSAT dead, fee
repealed; Kayrros survives by selling to oil & gas and finance — i.e. by *not* being the public-good
version).

## Whitespace: tried-and-failed vs. genuinely untried

**Tried and failed — do not re-enter:** carbon credit marketplaces · "AI climate risk platform for
enterprises" (Gro, Cervest, ClimateAi all died with the same pitch; survivors sell **specifically to
insurers and FIs**) · generic carbon accounting SaaS · voluntary biodiversity credit infrastructure ·
ocean CDR MRV · consumer/SMB carbon apps.

**Genuinely untried:**
- ⭐ **Small/medium utility and municipal tier for wildfire + vegetation risk.** Pano, Overstory and
  AiDash all sell to the *largest* utilities. There are **~3,000 US electric utilities**, most
  municipal or co-op, most unable to afford enterprise contracts, all facing the same liability.
  **The incumbents' own marketing (top-10 utilities, 15 utilities) confirms the long tail is
  unserved.** Best-supported whitespace in the report — it sits directly downstream of the category
  with the most proven buyers.
- **EUDR due-diligence for mid-market importers** — barely tried; the deadline slipped twice, which
  is exactly why incumbents under-invested. Delay risk is real.
- **Insurance-side parcel risk for hazards other than wildfire** — Faura expanded to five hazards on
  a $3.5M seed, suggesting a receptive buyer and an uncrowded category.
- **US municipal mid-market water utility AI** — no graveyard here, just small companies.

# Climate Adaptation & Risk — key findings

## ⭐ Top pick: broker-side wildfire risk score appeals

California created a **statutory right** — disclosure of your wildfire risk score plus an appeal
process — and **every vendor built for the carrier side.** ZestyAI (Z-FIRE), Delos ($9M Series A
led by HSBC Asset Management), Faura all sell to insurers. **No product exists for the homeowner or
the retail broker in an appeal.**

The homeowner has no idea which property attributes drove the score, no way to know which mitigation
buys the most score movement per dollar, and no structured way to assemble evidence a carrier's model
will ingest. **The insurer holds the model; the homeowner holds the photos.**

Forcing function is in force *and strengthening*: the Sustainable Insurance Strategy regulations took
effect 1 Jan 2025 permitting forward-looking cat models conditioned on writing 85% of statewide share
in distressed ZIPs; three Lara-sponsored laws took effect 1 Jan 2026 including **AB 1** (periodic
review of Safer from Wildfires) and **SB 429** (grants toward a *public* wildfire catastrophe model).

**The buyer is the broker, not the homeowner** — homeowners pay once under duress (bad recurring
revenue); brokers lose commission on every non-renewal they can't re-place and have recurring
budgets. Home-hardening contractors need lead generation as a second channel.

Inputs are free: CAL FIRE FHSZ, LANDFIRE, NAIP, county assessor parcel and roof data, IBHS criteria.

⚠️ **Conflict with the Regulation agent:** this agent reports statewide Zone 0 as *still a Board of
Forestry draft as of mid-2026* (San Diego adopted locally: new structures 28 Feb 2026, existing
28 Feb 2027). The Regulation agent reports it **approved 19 Aug 2026**. The fact-checker is
resolving this.

## Other findings

**Hazard mitigation plans — a free, precisely-targeted lead list.** ~**22% of the US population**
lives in a jurisdiction without a current FEMA-approved plan. Plans expire every five years, and
lapse means ineligibility for BRIC/HMGP. **FEMA publishes "Hazard Mitigation Plan Statuses – v1" via
OpenFEMA — a machine-readable list of every jurisdiction and its expiration date.** That is a rare
thing: a free, exact go-to-market list for a document-generation product. Risk: small municipal ACVs,
slow procurement.

**Distribution-level interconnection — the unglamorous, underserved half.** Everyone chases the
transmission queue because LBNL publishes clean data (median **>5 years** IR-to-COD; ~8,200 projects;
1,312 GW generation + 749 GW storage). **Nobody has normalized distribution interconnection rules,
screens, forms and hosting capacity maps across ~3,000 US utilities** because it is grinding
integration work. **The moat is accumulated janitorial data work rather than model IP** — which is
exactly the moat a small team can build.

**Building performance standards — best budget quality, but occupied.** Recurring, penalty-forced,
paid by private owners. NYC LL97 charges **$268/tCO2e over cap** plus $0.50/sf/month for late reports;
DC's first compliance period ends 31 Dec 2026 with penalties assessed 2027; Maryland's BEPS survived
federal preemption challenge (dismissed with prejudice 31 Mar 2026). **But the jurisdiction count is
heavily inflated by vendor marketing** — ACEEE (Jan 2026) counts **four states + DC + 12 localities**,
not the "40+ cities" blogs claim. Touchstone IQ, Measurabl, EnergyPrint, Vert Energy already occupy it.

**FEMA BRIC is the weakest budget story found.** Terminated April 2025, litigated by 20+ state AGs,
ruled unlawful, reinstated; the FY2024–25 $1B NOFO closed 23 July 2026. **A program that was
canceled, sued back into existence, and is now targeted for structural reduction is a bad foundation
for ARR.** FEMA is also actively *reducing* application burden (no full BCA under $1M, discount rate
cut 7%→3.1%) — the regulator is eating part of the market.

**Post-flood substantial damage determinations.** The 50% rule is an NFIP participation requirement
under 44 CFR 60.3, and getting it wrong risks every flood policy in the community. One or two staff
inspect hundreds to thousands of structures under time pressure. **But it's surge-driven, not
recurring** — desperate for six weeks, then nothing for three years.

## Already crowded — where a small team cannot win

**Climate risk analytics is the most crowded, best-capitalized space examined.** Moody's acquired RMS
in 2021; First Street, Jupiter, Verisk and ZestyAI have proprietary claims data and enterprise sales
motions. CarbonPlan asked nine climate-risk companies for the same fire and flood data on 342 real
addresses — **seven declined or didn't respond**, and the two that participated agreed on only about
**one in five properties in the LA area.** That disagreement does not create an opening for a *third*
opaque model; validation requires incumbent cooperation, which they refused.

Also avoid: transmission queue analytics (LBNL gives the data away) · benchmarking/Portfolio Manager
filing · carrier-side wildfire underwriting · anything requiring hardware.

> **The honest structural point:** a solo undergraduate's advantage is not modeling sophistication —
> it is willingness to do unglamorous data normalization across hundreds of fragmented jurisdictions
> that no VC-backed company finds worth the labor. Every good idea here has that shape.
