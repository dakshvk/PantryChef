## HEADLINE FINDING — a strategic reversal

**The US federal environmental compliance market is contracting, not growing, in 2026.**

Nearly every federal driver has been weakened, narrowed, or rescinded in the last 18 months. EU
drivers have been delayed and scope-cut by roughly half. The mandates *actually in force with money
attached right now* are concentrated in three places: **EU maritime, California state/local wildfire
and building code, and PFAS liability.**

This directly contradicts the working assumption behind several Wave 1 candidates. See "Conflicts
with Wave 1" below.

## ⚠️ CONFLICTS WITH WAVE 1 — for the CEO agent to resolve

**1. CEQA tooling.** The Land agent named CEQAnet + the AB 130/SB 131 exemption screener a Tier-1
opportunity. This agent notes the *same law* removes CEQA review triggers wholesale — less review,
less consulting work, smaller market. **Both are true.** The exemption-screening task is new and
real; the total pool of CEQA work is shrinking. Net effect unresolved.

**2. Wetland/§404 permitting workflow.** Ecobot proves consultants buy this. But WOTUS would cut
~81% of protected wetland acreage and explicitly reduce permits issued. **Do not build a company on
federal wetland permitting.**

**3. Phase I ESA survives this — and is strengthened by it.** Phase I ESAs are driven by **CERCLA
§101(35)(B)**, not the Clean Water Act or NEPA. CERCLA's PFOA/PFOS designation was just **upheld by
the D.C. Circuit on 18 Aug 2026**, expanding rather than contracting liability. The Phase I candidate
is the one Wave 1 Tier-1 item this report reinforces instead of undermining.

## Revenue paths ranked by real accessibility

**1. California local fire agencies, fire safe councils, special districts — MOST ACCESSIBLE**

The mechanism: under California's **SB/DVBE Option**, any state agency may contract **directly with a
California-certified Small Business between $5,000.01 and $249,999.99 with no competitive bid**, after
obtaining quotes from just two SBs. Certified SBs also get a 5% bid preference. Self-certify on
Cal eProcure in weeks.

**This is the one channel where "no past performance" is not disqualifying.**

Buyer pays for software today: **Fire Aside** serves 100+ fire agencies, fire safe councils and HOAs
across 12 states on annual subscription; Marin Wildfire line-items "Defensible Space Evaluation and
Inspection Software" as a funded project. **3Di Engage** competes.

Money is appropriated: **CAL FIRE Wildfire Prevention Grants up to $135M FY25–26** (Prop 4 + Climate
Investments), California Fire Foundation deploying **$13.8M** directly to local departments, CFSC
allows **up to $200,000** per applicant. Some agencies have their subscription paid by community
foundations rather than their own budget.

Range: $5k–$50k/yr per agency *(unverified — no vendor publishes pricing)*.

**2. Environmental consulting firms — accessible, small.** See verdict below. $1k–$25k/yr per firm,
self-serve tiers exist so you can close without procurement.

**3. Federal micro-purchases — accessible but slow.** Thresholds rose 1 Oct 2025: micro-purchase
**$10,000 → $15,000**, simplified acquisition **$250,000 → $350,000**. At or below MPT: no public
posting, no bid package, no protest risk, purchasable on a government card. MPT–SAT is **reserved for
small business**. Real barrier is finding a program officer with a card and a problem.

**4. Universities — accessible, tiny.** Software allowable on NSF awards under 2 CFR 200.403.
Practically $500–$5,000 line items decided by one PI. Good for logos, not revenue.

**5. SBIR/STTR — viable, but NOT a path to a first dollar.** Authority **lapsed 30 Sep 2025**,
restored 13 Apr 2026 as **PL 119-83** through 2031. Restart still working through — **no FY2026 NOAA
solicitation deadline posted as of Aug 2026.** NSF Phase I up to **$305,000** with the best success
rate (~20%); most agencies run 10–15% Phase I, 40–55% Phase I→II. At ~15% odds over a 6–9 month cycle,
expected value of a first proposal is roughly $15k–$45k for ~150 hours of writing. **Apply after you
have paying customers** — commercialization potential is a scored criterion.

**6. Utilities — high value, low accessibility.** AiDASH sizes utility vegetation management as a
**$24B expense** and has deployed across 800,000+ line-miles — and **Schneider Electric just agreed to
acquire it at $350M**. Formal RFPs, SOC 2, cyber review, references, rate-recovery prudency review,
12–24 month cycles. Not closeable by a new company inside 12 months.

**7. Insurance — real budgets, wrong buyer for you.** Jupiter, First Street, Moody's, S&P, CoreLogic
sell here. But underwriting data must survive model-governance review, and CarbonPlan found these
vendors **materially disagree with each other**, which is why buyers demand deep validation. A solo
founder cannot clear that gate.

**8. Conservation NGOs — least accessible, and the evidence is weak.** TNC/WCS/CI do buy enterprise
software — but every verified purchase is **back-office infrastructure from a major vendor** (Power BI,
SAS, Esri, New Relic), not field tools from a startup. **No evidence found of any NGO paying a
subscription to an early-stage conservation software company.** Field-tool culture runs on
grant-funded, donated, or open-source software. Nature-tech VC exceeded $2B in 2024 — the sector is
**investor-funded, not customer-funded.** Treat NGOs as design partners and distribution, never as
your revenue base.

## The consulting channel verdict

**Yes, it works — but it is a modest business, and the cannibalization worry is real but misdirected.**

*Evidence it works:* **Ecobot** is used by "the top AEC firms, natural resources consulting firms,
counties, state and federal DOTs, the mitigation banking industry," has submitted **3,500+ wetland
determination forms to USACE**, and openly markets that it **"cuts total workload time in half."**
Consultants buy a tool whose headline promise is fewer hours. **Transect** does the same for due
diligence — one customer replaced a **$5,000 one-off analysis** with unlimited automated reports.

*Why cannibalization matters less than it looks:* large firms run a mix of T&M and **fixed-price**
work — on fixed-price, every hour your tool removes is margin the firm keeps. The five largest firms
hold **51% of the US market** on **$11.3B** combined environmental/sustainability revenue.

*Where the objection is real:* specialized firms run **utilization targets near 80%**. A pure T&M shop,
or a PM bonused on utilization, will resist.

*The honest caveat:* **Ecobot has raised only ~$4.0–5.6M since ~2018.** That is not a company that
found an enormous market. And its demand substrate is shrinking (WOTUS, CEQA reform).

**Verdict:** sell to the **individual practitioner and small-to-mid regional firm**, self-serve,
$1k–$25k/yr, positioned as *win more bids with the same headcount* — never *reduce your hours*.
Do not build on federal wetland/NEPA permitting.

## FASTEST PATH TO FIRST DOLLAR

### Defensible-space / vegetation-compliance inspection software for California local fire agencies, sold as a Cal eProcure–certified Small Business.

1. **Brand-new mandate, hard date, no incumbent lock-in.** Zone 0 finalized **19 Aug 2026** — eight
   days before this research. VHFHSZ compliance **1 Jan 2027**. Every agency in a VHFHSZ must stand up
   inspection, tracking and resident-notification workflow within twelve months. You arrive as the
   requirement lands, not five years late.
2. **Procurement structurally favors you.** SB/DVBE Option: direct contract, no competitive bid, up to
   $249,999.99, two SB quotes. Self-certification takes weeks.
3. **Budget already appropriated — and the buyer doesn't always pay.** $135M CAL FIRE, $13.8M
   Fire Foundation direct to departments, $200k/applicant CFSC. Community foundations have paid for
   agencies' software outright.
4. **The buyer is one person you can reach.** A fire marshal or fire safe council director. No SOC 2
   gate, no model governance, no 18-month cycle.
5. **It is geographically yours.** Santa Barbara County is VHFHSZ. You can drive to your first ten
   customers. Local presence is an advantage in this channel and no other on this list.
6. **Validated but not consolidated.** Two players, hundreds of California agencies, and a brand-new
   regulation neither built their product around. Compare utility VM, where the leader just sold for $350M.

**90-day sequence:** self-certify on Cal eProcure → interview 15 fire marshals and fire safe council
directors across Santa Barbara, Ventura, SLO on how they'll actually operationalize Zone 0 → build the
narrowest tool solving the one thing they all name → land 2–3 paid pilots at $5k–$15k under SB/DVBE
with no bid → use those as the past-performance references that unlock everything else, including an
NSF SBIR in year two.

**Strategic frame:** federal environmental compliance demand is being actively dismantled and EU
corporate disclosure is delayed past your runway. **Wildfire is the exception** — the one environmental
domain where California is *adding* mandates and *increasing* appropriations, because the insurance
and liability crisis forces it regardless of federal politics.
