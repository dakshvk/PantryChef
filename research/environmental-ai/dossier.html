01 — What the fact-check destroyed

The front-runner does not survive

Seven of ten agents converged on California wildfire compliance. The scoring agent made it the single "start Monday" recommendation. Then an adversarial fact-checker re-probed every load-bearing claim, and two of the six pillars collapsed.

Refuted — Zone 0 is not law

The Board of Forestry voted 8–0 on 19 Aug 2026 to approve a final draft. OAL review has not happened. There is no statewide effective date and nothing is enforceable. A page titled literally "Approved Aug 19, 2026 — not yet law" is the top organic result on the subject.

Refuted — the compliance deadlines do not exist

There is no 1 Jan 2027 VHFHSZ deadline and no 1 Jan 2028 High-FHSZ deadline. The rule applies immediately to new construction on adoption; existing structures get Phase 1 within 3 years and Phase 2 within 5 years — both running from an effective date that does not yet exist.

The "twelve months to stand up a workflow" premise is off by a factor of three to five. "You arrive as the requirement lands, not five years late" is precisely inverted.

Refuted — the procurement advantage doesn't reach the buyer

California's SB/DVBE Option is real: direct contract, no competitive bid, up to $249,999.99 on two quotes. But it is a state-agency authority under Gov. Code §§14838.5/.7. Cities, counties, special districts and local fire agencies run their own purchasing codes. This was the "no past performance required" pillar — the single best reason the opportunity ranked first on fundability. It never applied to the recommended buyer.

Corrected — the incumbent is stronger than assessed

"100+ agencies across 12 states" is confirmed only as text on Fire Aside's own website. Real pricing surfaced: Berkeley's contract runs to a not-to-exceed $85,000 per year — far above the inferred $5k–$50k range. A pilot priced at $5–15k would underprice against an incumbent already clearing $85k at one mid-size city.

A founder who builds a 90-day plan around "every agency must operationalize Zone 0 by 1 Jan 2027" walks into fifteen customer interviews and is told the rule isn't in effect and nobody has a date. It is the kind of error a fire marshal catches instantly.Adversarial fact-check agent

The scoring agent had written its own escape hatch before it knew any of this. Under What would change my mind, item one: "Board of Forestry confirms Zone 0 is still a draft with no adopted compliance date → downgrade from 'start Monday' to 'one of four.'" That trigger fired. The demotion below is the agent's own rule applied to the fact-checker's evidence, not a second opinion.

02 — How to read every claim below

Verification status

The egress proxy blocked direct document fetches for every agent in this program. Not one agent opened a single primary PDF. Every quote is a search-index rendering of a real URL. Nothing was invented — every agent flagged this unprompted — but the distinction below is the whole point.

Confirmed

Independently corroborated across multiple distinct query framings and source domains during the fact-check pass.

Partial

Substantially right, but a material detail — a date, an amount, an attribution — is wrong. The correction is stated inline.

Refuted

Evidence contradicts the claim, or exact-phrase probes returned nothing at all. Do not use.

Unverified

Reached this page from a single agent's search summary and was never independently re-probed. Treat as a hypothesis.

03 — What five independent literatures agree on

The convergent signal

These themes were named by unrelated fields with no shared authors. Repetition across non-overlapping literatures is the strongest evidence this program produced — stronger than any single finding.

SIGNAL 01

Annotation labour is the universal bottleneck

Coral, camera traps, aerial surveys, bioacoustics, terrestrial ML and marine AI each independently name manual annotation or labelled-data scarcity as the binding constraint. Six literatures, one sentence.

⚠ The headline statistic once attached to this — "200 full-time days per million images" — returned zero corroboration across three probes. Refuted

SIGNAL 02

Data fragmentation across incompatible systems

The Water Quality Portal holds ~25M records of which ~14.5M have missing or ambiguous metadata — 130 distinct parameter names for nitrate-plus-nitrite alone. Federal fuel treatments, ocean FAIR data, microplastics and biodiversity all report the identical structural complaint.

✓ Confirmed — but the source is Sprague et al. 2017, nine years old, not a current finding. Partial

SIGNAL 03

Models break when moved to a new site

BirdNET's predictive power runs 0.16–0.23 in North America, Oceania and Europe but 0.03–0.04 in Africa and Asia. Four literatures name domain shift. Nobody sells operational benchmarking for environmental ML.

✓ BirdNET figures confirmed exactly. MegaDetector's time-lapse gap corroborated only as "~60%", and it is a trigger-mode effect, not generalization. Confirmed

SIGNAL 04

Cost, not capability, gates adoption

A practitioner survey names high cost as the main barrier across occupations. Coral monitoring is limited by "expensive equipment or substantial expert time"; soil carbon is "prohibitively expensive."

→ Commercial rule: sell the removal of an expert salary or a capex line. Never sell an addition.

SIGNAL 05

The gap between reported and real

Only 2.6% of the ocean is implemented and highly or fully protected, against far larger headline coverage claims. Fuel treatments can't be evaluated. NEPA produces no structured record of itself.

✓ Confirmed, peer-reviewed — Pike et al. 2024, Conservation Letters. Every one of these is a document-processing problem hiding inside a policy problem. Confirmed

THE META-THESIS

Don't build the model

The technical bottlenecks are increasingly solved by open-source tools and open models. The deployment bottleneck — a small organisation with no ML staff, no GPU and no budget getting an answer this week — is not.

→ Build the operational layer that makes existing open models usable by people who will never install VIAME.

Categories that never had a buyer

This is the finding that killed the most emotionally appealing opportunities in the corpus, including the one with the founder's only non-transferable geographic advantage.

  • Biodiversity credits. Of ~11.6 million credits issued or projected, 124,183 sold and 1,285 retired — ever. The market is roughly 20 transactions, mostly under $50k. Surveyed participants report the credits serve "marketing and branding" with no direct commercial benefit.
  • Conservation technology. Ai2 describes its own Skylight — the leading IUU-fishing platform — as a "zero-revenue AI for the environment program" whose API is "not intended for commercial use." Global Fishing Watch is free. Wild Me, the most technically successful conservation AI project ever built, had to merge into another nonprofit.
  • NGOs as customers. Every verified NGO software purchase is back-office infrastructure from a major vendor — Power BI, SAS, Esri. No evidence was found of any NGO paying a subscription to an early-stage conservation software company. Nature-tech VC exceeded $2B in 2024: the sector is investor-funded, not customer-funded.

This is not an unbuilt market

It is a market where the best-funded, best-engineered players deliberately give the product away, because nobody will pay. The price has already been set at zero. Scoring that as whitespace would be a category error.

Round-two verification: the deadline held, the market didn't

✓ Confirmed: the 1 Nov 2027 date, 5 samples per school / 2 per childcare, ≥20% of facilities per year, and the two-documented-attempts outreach rule — all corroborated by 3+ independent sources including EPA's own factsheets. The mandated audit artifact is real.

✓ The litigation risk was overstated and mis-aimed. AWWA v. EPA has never stayed the rule; argument is set for 30 Sept 2026; and AWWA is attacking the lead service line replacement mandate — not §141.92 school provisions. The likeliest adverse ruling doesn't touch this opportunity.

✗ But "no vendor does this" is false. 120Water ships a School & Daycare Sampling product, a Facilities product, an LCRI product, and a state-agency Lead-in-Facilities app — and ran a 2025 webinar specifically on this requirement, with statewide references including the Indiana Finance Authority and Chicago Public Schools.

✗ The buyer thesis is inverted. Rural Water Associations don't buy software — they endorse it. 120Water already holds the NRWA partnership slot nationally plus six state associations, with preferred pricing for 31,000+ member utilities, framed explicitly around Lead and Copper compliance. ✗ And EPA partially commoditised the wedge two months ago. Its June 2026 List Resource Guide tells utilities to build the roster from billing records, service line inventories, and existing state facility inventories — not spatial modelling. A utility knows whom it bills, and a billing file is stronger legal evidence than a polygon intersection.

What actually survives: the 50-state licensed-childcare data asset — normalised, geocoded, continuously maintained. California and Texas publish bulk CSV; New York excludes all of NYC; Florida offers a search UI only. That is real moat material, plus the genuine entity-resolution problem billing records fail at: a daycare inside a church, strip mall or apartment complex on someone else's meter. Reframe from product to data layer — sold into 120Water, Trinnex and BlueConduit rather than against them.

3

Zone 0 defensible-space inspection — demoted

California local fire agencies  2 pillars refuted

7.70as scored
What survives
Defensible-space inspection is an in-force obligation under PRC 4291 that agencies perform today — which is exactly why Fire Aside already has paying customers. The market is real with or without the 0–5 ft rule. Verified second driver: 10 CCR §2644.9 forces insurers using wildfire risk in pricing to discount for documented mitigation, and the FAIR Plan stood up 12 discrete discounts effective 15 Nov 2025, up to 16.4%. $135M in CAL FIRE prevention grants exists.
What broke
The urgency was fabricated (no effective date, 3–5 year phase-in). The procurement advantage never applied to local agencies. The incumbent charges $85k/yr at one city, not the inferred $5–50k. The FY25–26 CAL FIRE window closed 6 Aug 2025, grantees are agencies not vendors, and software eligibility is unestablished.
Where it lands
A credible but ordinary opportunity with a slow-burning tailwind and a funded incumbent — not a twelve-month window. It keeps its scored 7.70 because the scoring agent already gave it the lowest whitespace mark in the top eight; what it loses is the "start Monday" slot.

Round-two verification: no window, and the budget mostly isn't there

The clock hasn't started. Eight days after the vote, Zone 0 still had not been submitted to OAL. The compliance clocks run from Secretary of State publication — so Phase 1 for existing structures is ~late 2029 and Phase 2 ~late 2031. There is no enforcement cliff in the next twelve months. Berkeley, the most aggressive jurisdiction in California, adopted in April 2025 and still slipped its own inspections from January to May 2026.

Scope resolved: Zone 0 reaches the State Responsibility Area plus Very High FHSZ in the Local Responsibility Area. "High" FHSZ is not covered — that appears only in the separate AB 38 point-of-sale disclosure statute.

The $85,000 figure was mis-attributed. Berkeley's actual Fire Aside contract is $60,000 over two years — about $30k/yr — with an option to extend to $160,000 total. The $85,000 belonged to Tablet Command, a different product. Grass Valley bought Fire Aside for $11,660.80 and needed a budget adjustment from a local sales-tax measure to afford it.

And the decisive finding: in the largest multi-agency deployment on record — the entire Lake Tahoe Basin — the fire agencies didn't pay at all. The Parasol Tahoe Community Foundation and the Tahoe Fund bought it for them. A market where a philanthropist buys the software for the customer is a market with no software budget.

Plus a free competitor: OSFM publishes a "Model Defensible Space Inspection platform" so jurisdictions can build their own, and CAL FIRE already runs its inspection intake on Esri Survey123. Every prospect's alternative costs $0 on a licence they already hold.

On the incumbent: Fire Aside is 18 people, seed-stage, no Series A — beatable on paper. But it is already deployed in Berkeley (first Zone 0 ordinance in the state, with a formal implementation plan) and Marin (a JPA with $24.1M/yr and a dedicated parcel tax), and it already captures the parcel attributes, photos and GPS that Zone 0 requires. Zone 0 is a form change for them, not a product build. Meanwhile 3Di claims more defensible-space inspections than any other SaaS nationwide. Of 838 California fire departments, 212 are all-volunteer — the serviceable set is a few hundred, and Fire Aside claims 100+ agencies already.

The gap
SMARTS is public and is literally the mechanism plaintiffs use to select targets for Clean Water Act citizen suits. A facility with a rolling exceedance pattern is visible to LA Waterkeeper before it is visible to its own plant manager. A small, identifiable set of repeat filers drives most California filings, so filer behaviour is learnable — and §505 requires a 60-day Notice of Intent, a fixed exploitable warning window.
Why it scores
The purest expression of the graveyard thesis in the corpus: the buyer is compelled by strict liability, and you sell into a litigation-avoidance budget, which is categorically larger than a compliance-software budget. And the target list is the dataset — you can generate a ranked outbound list of at-risk facilities for free, which solves the no-sales-relationships problem better than anything else here.
The buyer
The EHS or plant manager at a permitted facility with a live exceedance pattern — metal recyclers, scrap yards, ports, logistics, ready-mix. Second buyer: outside environmental counsel at regional defence firms, who will resell it.
First 90 days
Build the exceedance-pattern and filer-behaviour scoring engine, backtest it against 2023–25 notices already filed, publish the accuracy number, then cold-email the top 100 at-risk facilities with their own free score.

Round-two verification: the last idea standing — and the count is one letter away

The premise of the question was wrong, and that matters. The prior agent's "zero results" was not a search failure — the State Water Board does not publish notice counts. It ran a single tracking exercise covering 60 notices from 2009–2010 and published it in 2011. Nothing since.

But the filer ecosystem is unambiguously alive and expanding. The Ninth Circuit decided Inland Empire Waterkeeper v. Corona Clay on 5 Aug 2026 — affirming citizen-plaintiff standing and holding that "waters of the United States" is a merits question, not jurisdictional, which keeps cases alive past dismissal. San Diego Coastkeeper sued SeaWorld in March 2025. LA Waterkeeper is actively hiring a Stormwater Compliance Manager. A new entrant, Pacific Environmental Advocates, has appeared. SCOTUS declined to narrow citizen suits in June 2025.

The economics support a subscription. Average California consent decree exceeds $79,000; typical range $75k–$250k; one documented decree ran $325,000, of which $225,500 went to the plaintiff org and its lawyers. Statutory exposure is $68,445 per violation per day. A facility facing that can rationally pay several thousand a year to avoid it. There are ~14,000 IGP permittees.

The data is genuinely usable — the strongest technical finding in the program. Bulk CSV on California's open-data portal: a 660 MB industrial monitoring file, plus violations, enforcement actions and inspections, plus a queryable ArcGIS REST layer. ⚠️ One caveat: some resource timestamps rendered as 2020. Verify refresh cadence before building.

⚠️ The medium-term threat: EPA's proposed "Prior Notice of Citizen Suits" rule (Federal Register, 24 Feb 2026) would create a free public centralised repository of notices — precisely the historical filer-behaviour corpus this product's moat depends on.

The move: file a California Public Records Act request with the State Water Board Office of Enforcement for all CWA §505 notices received 2023–2026. The Board is statutorily copied on every one of them. That single letter converts the largest unknown in this entire program into fact — and it is the dataset. If it returns 600 notices, you have the market size and the training corpus in one motion.

07 — What died and why

Rejected

The negative findings are worth as much as the positive ones. Each of these was seriously considered and killed by specific evidence.

Temperate reef & kelp forest CV

The most painful cut. Founder fit 10/10 — SBC LTER, PISCO, Channel Islands KFM, Reef Check, CINMS and NCEAS all within miles of UCSB, 40+ years of overlapping public data, four incompatible protocols nobody has crosswalked. It dies on one factor: the leading platforms in this space are given away free by their own builders. Build it on weekends for the marine credential; don't make it the company.

CEQA / CEQAnet tooling

AB 130/SB 131 creates a new hand-done exemption test by removing the much larger task it substitutes for. Projects clearing the screen generate no EIR, no scope, and no mitigation monitoring. A new compliance task created by a deregulatory statute is not a market — the statute's purpose is to reduce total work.

Landfill methane discrepancy ranking

Superb data story — the mandated method achieves 67% rate coverage at closed sites but 17% at open sites. But the methane fee was repealed by CRA in March 2025, the Waste Emissions Charge is barred until 2034, and MethaneSAT went dark. A ranking of who is under-reporting is a press release, not a product.

Consumer "safe to buy from" app

GoodGuide built exactly this — 175,000 products, all three dimensions — and was dismantled and killed in 2020. The labour data does not exist at barcode resolution. And the closer: ByHeart. A clean-ingredient scorer would have ranked the challenger brand above Abbott right up to its recall. The core promise is unfulfillable in your own motivating example.

Hazard mitigation plan generation

A rare, free, exact go-to-market list — FEMA publishes every jurisdiction's plan expiry date, with ~22% of the US population uncovered. Dead on money: BRIC was terminated, litigated, reinstated and is now targeted for reduction — and FEMA is actively reducing application burden, so the regulator is eating the market.

Carbon markets & offset MRV

Total 2024 VCM transaction value $535M — smaller than a single Series C — with volume down 25%. Nori dead. Running Tide dead with Microsoft, Shopify and Stripe as customers. Biodiversity credits: 1,285 retired ever.

Carbon-aware scheduling

The clearest do-not-build in the corpus. Electricity Maps discontinued its own marginal-emissions product in 2025 on veracity grounds, and US and EU legislation now prohibits its use in reporting. Regulation moved against the value case.

EPR & California textile SB 707

Good problem, wrong sales motion — enterprise procurement with no references — and the regulations don't bite until 1 July 2028, past the runway. Revisit in 18 months.

File a California Public Records Act request with the State Water Board Office of Enforcement for all Clean Water Act §505 60-day notices received 2023–2026.

09 — The side question

An app for companies safe to buy from

Asked separately: does anything combine environmental responsibility, labour practices and genuine product health in one place? No — and the reason is instructive.

GoodGuide built exactly this product — 175,000 products, 5,000 companies, rated on health, environment and social. UL bought it in 2012, stripped the social and environmental dimensions in 2016, and shut it down on 1 June 2020. Buycott, purpose-built for brand→parent mapping, has been dormant since 2016. Free2Work, Ethical Barcode and RESET are all defunct.

The survivors all narrowed to stay alive. Yuka does health only. Good On You does people and planet but only in fashion and beauty. Ethical Consumer does both across categories but is UK-only, paywalled, and has no scanner.

The structural blocker: the labour data does not exist at product resolution. KnowTheChain — the most credible public forced-labour benchmark — covers ~145 companies on a two-year refresh. Sourcemap has real traceability but it is proprietary enterprise data, locked per customer. Any app claiming a per-barcode labour score is inferring, not knowing.

The argument that ends it

ByHeart. The clean-ingredient challenger brand recalled all product in November 2025 in an infant botulism outbreak. An ingredient-list scorer would have ranked it above Abbott right until the recall. If the core promise is "we know which is safer," and the honest answer in your own motivating example is nobody could have known, the promise is unfulfillable at inception.

The one defensible fragment: a corporate-accountability layer no scanner has. Yuka reads ingredients; nothing reads litigation, recalls, enforcement actions and parent-company ownership. FDA recalls, class-action settlements, CPSC notices and UFLPA entity listings are public, structured, dated and change weekly. That is entity resolution over messy public filings — not "an LLM reads sustainability reports," which just launders marketing into scores.

Method. Ten agents across three waves: six discovery agents (problem scout, green-AI footprint, ocean & diving, land & terrestrial, research-gap hunter, ethical products), two market agents (funding & graveyard, regulation & procurement), an adversarial fact-checker, and a scoring agent. Every agent was required to cite source URLs, flag unverified claims explicitly, and end with a self-critique naming its own weakest findings.

The constraint that shaped everything. The network egress proxy blocked direct document fetches for every agent. No primary PDF was opened by anyone. Quotes are search-index renderings of real URLs — nothing was fabricated, and every agent disclosed this unprompted — but the fact-check pass exists precisely because search summaries are not sources.

The scoring agent finished before the fact-check reached it. Its original recommendation was Zone 0. The demotion on this page applies the agent's own stated decision rule — "Board of Forestry confirms Zone 0 is still a draft → downgrade from 'start Monday' to 'one of four'" — to the fact-checker's finding. It is not a second opinion; it is the agent's own logic completed.
