# Restaurant Discovery Interview

**Purpose:** find out where an independent restaurant actually loses money — *without* assuming it's
food waste, and *without* pitching anything.

---

## THE THREE RULES

These come from Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test*, and breaking them is how founders get politely
lied to for six months.

**1. Ask about their life, not your idea.** The moment you describe a product, everyone becomes
encouraging and the data is worthless. **Do not mention software, an app, or PantryChef at any point.**
If asked, say: *"I'm a UCSB stats student studying how independent kitchens manage cost. I'm not
selling anything."* That is true, and it is the best door-opener you have.

**2. Ask about specific things that already happened, never about the future.** "Would you use…?"
and "Do you think…?" produce fantasy. "Walk me through the last time…" produces facts.

**3. Chase numbers and past actions.** *Did they spend money on it? Did they spend time on it? Have
they tried to fix it before?* If none of those are true, they don't have a problem — they have an
opinion.

**Practical:** 20–25 minutes. Ask permission to record. Interview between 2–4pm — never during
service. Buy something. Talk less than 30% of the time. Silence is your best tool; let them fill it.

---

## PART 1 — Who you're talking to *(2 min)*

Segment them, because the answer changes everything downstream.

1. What type of place is this, and how many covers on a busy night?
2. How long have you been open?
3. How many locations?
4. **Who does the ordering — you, or someone else?** *(If not them, you may be talking to the wrong person. Ask who does and whether you can meet them.)*
5. **Who signs off on money — a new supplier, a new tool, a new hire?** *(This is the budget-authority question. Ask it early so you don't waste 20 minutes on someone who can't buy.)*

---

## PART 2 — The open question *(5 min — the most important section)*

**Do not narrow this. Do not mention cost, waste, or food. Let them tell you.**

6. **Walk me through yesterday. What went wrong?**
7. **What's the most frustrating part of running this place that has nothing to do with customers?**
8. **If you could wave a wand and fix one thing about how this kitchen runs, what would it be?**
9. **What keeps you up at night about the business?**

> **Write down what they say verbatim, in their words.** If food cost or waste doesn't come up
> unprompted in this section, that is your single most important data point — and it means the pivot
> thesis is wrong. Note it and keep going anyway.

---

## PART 3 — Where the money actually goes *(5 min)*

10. Roughly what percentage of revenue goes to food? To labour? *(Watch whether they know. Hesitation is data.)*
11. **Do you know your prime cost?** *(Food + labour. If they don't know the term, that tells you a lot about the sophistication of the buyer.)*
12. Which of those has moved most in the last year?
13. **Of everything that eats your margin — food, labour, rent, delivery apps, card fees, insurance — which one has actually cost you the most sleep this year?**
14. **What share of your orders come through delivery apps?** *(The published research has a ceiling — "up to 30%" — but no average. Nobody knows this number for independents. Every answer you collect is genuinely new data, and it decides whether commission or food cost is the bigger bleed for this specific restaurant.)*
15. **And what percentage do they take?** How do you feel about that? *(Ask for the effective rate including any marketing or sponsored-listing fee — the headline commission understates it.)*
16. Has anything surprised you on a P&L in the last six months?

> **Q14 × Q15 is a single multiplication that can end the interview's thesis.** 20% delivery mix on
> $1.2M at a 28% effective take is **$67,000/yr — more than the entire annual profit, and roughly
> triple the food-waste opportunity.** If that math lands for most operators you talk to, the
> restaurant pivot is aimed at the second-biggest problem.

> **Question 13 is the whole interview in one line.** If ten operators answer "delivery commissions"
> or "labour," your product idea is aimed at the wrong problem — and you'll have learned that for the
> price of ten coffees instead of a year of building.

---

## PART 4 — How costing actually works *(5 min)*

Only now do you get specific. Notice this is all *past behaviour*, not opinion.

17. **Walk me through how you priced the newest thing on your menu.** *(Let them talk. Do not help.)*
18. Do you know what each dish costs you to make? How do you know that?
19. **When was the last time you re-costed a dish?** What made you do it?
20. Your supplier's prices moved this year — how did you find out, and what did you do about it?
21. **Is there anything on the menu you suspect you're losing money on?** How would you know?
22. How long does it take you to count inventory, and how often do you do it?
23. Do you compare what you *should* have used against what you *actually* used? *(Theoretical vs actual variance. If yes, you've found a sophisticated operator. If they've never heard of it, note that.)*

---

## PART 5 — Waste, asked last and asked carefully *(4 min)*

**Deliberately at the end**, so it doesn't contaminate Parts 2–4. And note it's asked as observation,
not as accusation.

24. **What ended up in the bin at the end of last week?** *(Concrete, recent, specific.)*
25. What's the thing that spoils most often here?
26. **When something spoils, do you find out about it — or does it just disappear?**
27. Roughly what does that cost you in a month? *(If they have no idea, that's the answer.)*
28. Have you ever tried to do anything about it? What happened? *(Past attempted solutions = proof of real pain. No attempt = no pain.)*
29. Does anyone make you track or report waste? *(Probing for a regulatory forcing function.)*

---

## PART 6 — What they already pay for *(4 min)*

30. What software do you run this place on? *(Get every name — POS, scheduling, accounting, inventory, delivery.)*
31. **Roughly what does all of that cost you a month?** *(This establishes the price ceiling for anything new. The published research says the whole wallet is around $333/mo. Test it.)*
32. **Has your POS company ever tried to sell you extra modules — inventory, costing, scheduling? Did you take any?**
   *(This is the bundling question, and it is the one that decides whether a standalone tool has a
   prayer. Toast ships xtraCHEF; Square launched an inventory product with MarketMan in April 2026 at
   $99/mo. If operators say "yes, and I switched it on," a standalone product is dead on arrival for
   them. If they say "they pitched it and I ignored it," ask why.)*
33. What's the most useless thing you pay for?
34. **When was the last time you added a new tool? Who sold it to you and how did they find you?**
   *(This is your go-to-market answer, from the customer's mouth.)*
35. Is there anything you've tried and stopped using? Why?

---

## PART 7 — The close *(2 min)*

36. **If I came back in a month with something that showed you X, would that be worth anything to you?**
   *(Fill in X with whatever *they* said in Part 2 — not what you wanted them to say.)*
37. **Who else should I talk to?** *(Always ask. Referrals are how you get to twenty interviews.)*
38. Can I come back and show you what I find?

---

## SCORING: what the answers mean

Fill this in immediately after each interview, before you forget.

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Food cost or waste named **unprompted** in Part 2 | ✅ Strong. The thesis survives. |
| Only named after you asked in Part 4/5 | ⚠️ Weak. It's a real cost but not a felt pain. |
| **They have already tried to fix it** (spreadsheet, consultant, software) | ✅ **Strongest possible signal.** They spent money or time on this. |
| They can quote a number for waste or plate cost | ✅ They're measuring, so they care |
| They shrug and say "it's just part of the business" | ❌ Not a fundable problem |
| Q13 answered "delivery apps" or "labour" | 🔄 **Pivot the pivot.** Follow that instead. |
| **Q14 × Q15 exceeds $40K/yr** | 🔄 Commission is their real bleed, whatever they said in Part 2 |
| Q31 total software spend under ~$200/mo | ⚠️ Low ceiling; price accordingly |
| **Q32: they already switched on their POS's inventory module** | ❌ **Hardest no in the deck.** A standalone tool has to beat a bundled one they're already paying for. |
| Q5 shows they can't authorise spending | ❌ Wrong person — get the name of the right one |

### The kill criteria — decide these *now*, before you're emotionally invested

After **fifteen** interviews:

- **Fewer than 5 name food cost or waste unprompted** → the pivot is wrong. Follow whatever they *did* name.
- **Fewer than 3 have ever tried to fix it** → it's a cost they've accepted, not a problem they'll pay to solve.
- **Nobody can put a number on it** → you can't sell a solution to a problem nobody measures.
- **Most say delivery commissions are the real bleed** → that's the actual business. Go look at it.
- **More than 5 already run their POS's own inventory module** → you are selling against a bundle at a
  price you can't match. Take the costing product to bakeries, caterers and meal-prep companies
  instead, where there is no POS to bundle it into.

> **Write the kill criteria on paper and stick them above your desk before interview one.** The whole
> point of doing fifteen interviews is to be *allowed to be wrong* cheaply. Founders skip this step,
> hear what they hoped for in three of fifteen conversations, and build anyway.

## WHERE TO FIND FIFTEEN RESTAURANTS

You're in Santa Barbara, which is unusually good for this — a dense independent restaurant scene in
walking distance, and a student identity that opens doors a salesperson's wouldn't.

- **Walk in at 2:30pm on a Tuesday.** Slowest hour of the slowest day. Ask for the owner or chef.
- **Start with places you already eat at.** Familiarity converts.
- **Ask every single one for two referrals** (Q35). Fifteen interviews from five cold walk-ins is normal.
- **Independent, owner-operated, single location** is your target — one person makes the decision and
  holds the card. Skip chains and franchises; the decision is made elsewhere.
- Try the SBCC culinary programme and any local restaurant association for warm introductions.

**Cost: about $60 in coffee and one week.** Compare that against building for a year.
