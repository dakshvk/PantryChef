## THE FOUNDING FACT

**A typical US independent restaurant does ~$1.2M revenue at 3–6% net = roughly $48,000/year of
profit.** A $500K-revenue restaurant keeps $15,000–$50,000.

> Every dollar of software spend competes directly against a ~$48K pool. Remember this when
> pricing anything.

And the segment is shrinking: **independent restaurants fell 2.3% in 2025** — a net loss of ~9,500
locations, with full-service independents down 2.6% (net −6,400).

---

## MYTH KILLED: "90% fail in year one"

**It's false and traceable to nothing.** H.G. Parsa (Ohio State) reviewed the literature and found
no evidence for a 90% figure anywhere. When American Express was asked in writing for the data
behind its own 90% claim, **it said it could not provide any.**

Actual: **~17% fail in year one** — *lower* than the 19% first-year rate for all other service
businesses. Parsa's longitudinal study found 26% first-year, 57–61% cumulative over three years,
never exceeding 60%. ~49% within five years.

**"Restaurants are dying" is not a defensible framing for a pitch.**

## RANKED CAUSES OF MONEY LOSS

Converted to dollars/year for a $1.2M independent full-service restaurant with ~$48K net.

### 1. Labour — biggest by far, and moving against them

- **36.5% of sales in 2025**, above the 30–35% target band
- **Turnover is the hidden line.** Industry average >75% in 2025 vs 47% nationally; limited-service
  hourly **110%**, full-service hourly **92%** (Q3 2025)
- Replacement cost **$1,056 FOH / $1,491 BOH / $2,611 manager**; Black Box puts one hourly
  replacement at **$2,706**. A 50-employee restaurant at 80% turnover: **>$400,000/year**
- Wage floor ratcheting: CA state minimum **$16.90** (Jan 2026), LA $17.87, West Hollywood $20.25,
  $20/hr fast food at 60+ unit chains, **no tip credit in California**
- A 4% labour increase needs a 2.5–3.2% menu price rise to hold margin

### 2. Delivery commissions — can be the entire net profit

- DoorDash 15/25/30% by tier · Uber Eats 15–30% · Grubhub 5–20% base **plus** 10–20% marketing fee
  to appear in search
- **Effective 30–40% of order revenue** once sponsored listings are counted
- *"Most independents stop making money on delivery when effective commission exceeds 25%"*
- **80% of independents still rely on third-party delivery**; can be up to 30% of total revenue
- **Math: 15% of $1.2M = $180K delivery × 25% effective = $45,000/yr — approximately the entire net
  profit.** At a 30% delivery mix it's catastrophic.
- Independents lack the scale to negotiate the rates chains get. Structural, not operational.

### 3. Theft — the biggest claimed number, and the worst-sourced

Widely recycled: 75% of shrinkage is theft, ~$20B/yr ≈ 4% of sales; QSR employee theft ~7%.
At $1.2M that's $48,000 — again, the whole profit.

⚠️ **Treat as marketing until proven otherwise.** No date, no methodology, no primary NRA
publication found. It circulates exclusively through loss-prevention vendors, CPA marketing, and a
polygraph-services website. **Do not build a comparison on it.**

### 4. Food cost / waste — real, bounded, smaller than the above

- **4–10% of purchased inventory never reaches a customer.** At 30% food cost on $1.2M = $360K
  purchases → **$14,400–$36,000/yr**
- **But most of that band is unavoidable** — trim loss, cooking yield, bones, peels. The recoverable
  slice is the top of the range, not the whole thing.
- Theoretical-vs-actual variance benchmark: **<1% excellent, 1–2% normal, 2–3% investigate, >5%
  systemic.** A 2-point AvT gap on $1.2M ≈ **$24,000/yr**

### 5–8. Undercapitalisation · rent · insurance · card fees

Poor financial management is the top *failure* cause (distinct from margin loss) — opening costs
$175K–$750K and most need 2–3 years to real break-even. Rent is fixed and non-addressable by
software. Liability insurance up 5–25%, liquor liability up 25–40% in some regions.

## What operators themselves say — NRA 2026

Biggest challenge, H1 2026:
- **Recruiting/retaining staff — 32.9%** *(surged from 18% at the start of 2026)*
- **Food costs — 29.7%**

Together nearly two-thirds of all responses. **87% reported food cost increases.** ~90% of
full-service operators raised menu prices.

> **Food cost is the #2 stated pain, named by ~30% of operators. That's genuine demand signal — but
> labour beat it.**

## IS FOOD COST ACTUALLY THE PROBLEM? — the verdict

**Real and top-3, but not the biggest — and the slice a constraint engine addresses is smaller than
the food-waste literature implies.**

**Four arguments against the waste framing:**

**(a) The headline number is mostly not your waste.** The much-quoted **$162B** restaurant food waste
figure (Georgetown Portion Balance Coalition, Mar 2026) contains its own refutation: ~17% of meals go
uneaten and **nearly 70% of that is what customers leave on the plate.** Plate waste is a portion-size
and menu-design problem, not an inventory problem. **A constraint engine touches at most ~30% of the
number everyone quotes.**

**(b) Labour is bigger and moving faster.** 36.5% vs food's ~30%, above its historical band while food
is roughly in-band. **There is no legislature raising your beef price by decree; there is one raising
your wage floor.**

**(c) Delivery commission can exceed the entire waste opportunity in one line** — $45K vs $14–36K,
most of which is unavoidable. And commission is **visible** on a weekly statement. Waste is invisible,
which is why it's under-prioritised and why it's hard to sell against.

**(d) The category is saturated and the incumbents cost more than the pain.** MarginEdge $350/location/mo;
Restaurant365 Essential $469–499/location/mo. That's **$4,200–6,000/yr against a $48,000 profit —
9–12% of net profit for software.** They already do invoice capture, recipe costing, plate cost and
AvT variance.

### Where the case DOES hold up

- **The leverage is genuinely high.** 1 point of food cost on $1.2M = **$12,000/yr = 25% of net
  profit.** Closing a 2-point AvT gap ≈ $24K ≈ half the year's profit.
- **Operators name it themselves** — 29.7%, and 87% saw increases.
- ⭐ **Static recipe costs are demonstrably broken by 2024–26 volatility.** Ground beef hit a record
  **$6.23/lb (Sept 2025)**; beef/veal inflation **15% YoY (Dec 2025)** with the US cattle herd at its
  smallest in 60+ years; eggs ran from $1.33/doz to **$6.23 peak (Mar 2025)** — a 368% move — then
  fell 34% YoY by Jan 2026; coffee projected **+19–25%** on tariffs and weather.
  **A menu costed in January is wrong by March.**

### The recommended pitch

> **Do not build the pitch on waste-reduction ROI — the numbers won't survive a skeptical operator's
> scrutiny.** The defensible version is **dynamic re-costing under price volatility**:
> *"Your beef went up 15%. Here are the six dishes now underwater, and here's what to run instead."*
> That's where the constraint engine is genuinely differentiated and where 2024–26 conditions make
> the pain acute and recurring.

## How costing is done today — badly, or not at all

- **42% of restaurants still use spreadsheets or pen-and-paper** for critical back-of-house operations
- **~1 in 5 rely on "paper and in-person chats, sticky notes, and gut instincts"**
- **Inventory counts: 52% weekly, 26% monthly** — from a survey of **11,240 restaurant and bar
  workers**, the best-sampled datapoint in this entire research

**The re-costing gap — this is the actual opportunity:**

| | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Supplier prices change | **every 2–3 weeks per product** |
| Operators re-cost the menu | **every 90–180 days** |

> **A 6–20× mismatch in refresh rate.** "Operators on Excel re-cost once a quarter and find out at
> month-end that they've been losing margin for ten weeks."

## Who hurts most vs who can pay — these are different people

| | Feels the pain | Has budget | Friction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single-location independent** | Most acutely | **~$48K/yr total profit** — $4,200/yr software = ~9% of it | Highest. Price-sensitive to refusal. |
| **Small chain (2–10 units)** | Real, visible across units | Yes — R365/MarginEdge's actual market | Ops person, expects integrations, longer cycle |
| **Franchise** | Least — corporate mandates the stack | Corporate's, not theirs | Effectively closed |

**Segment sizes:** 412,498 independents (~70% of all US locations) · **152,430 single-location
full-service restaurants** · 16,718 new restaurants opened 2025, of which **14,750 (88.2%) were
independents** — new openings are a motivated greenfield entry point.

**Highest-pain segments:** independent full-service single location · **fine dining** (highest food
cost 30–38%, highest prime cost, most perishables, most volatility exposure) · wine-forward concepts ·
beef-heavy concepts.
**Lowest pain:** pizza (22–28% food cost, five-ingredient menus — a spreadsheet genuinely suffices)
and QSR chains.

**The chef-owner** is the ideal buyer — one person is chef, owner, purchaser and menu author, so no
committee and no procurement. ⚠️ But no data was found quantifying what share of independents are
chef-owned.

> **The uncomfortable structural read:** the segment that hurts most has the least money and is
> shrinking 2.6%/year. The segment with budget is exactly where R365 and MarginEdge are entrenched.
> "Sell to independents at a price they can afford" and "build a business" are in tension at a $48K
> customer profit pool.

## ⚠️ SOURCE QUALITY WARNING

A large share of food-cost benchmarks come from companies selling inventory software — Restaurant365,
MarginEdge, MarketMan, Toast, meez, Supy, Apicbase, WISK, Altametrics, Sculpture Hospitality. They
have a direct commercial interest in making food cost sound large and mismanagement universal.

**Worse:** a distinct cluster of 2026-dated sites recurring throughout these results —
`restaurantinventorytools.com`, `vantainsights.com`, `culta.ai`, `stockcount.io`, `dishcost.com`,
`cucinovo.com`, `vellinapp.com`, `useforcs.com`, `nommy.app`, `inntally.com` — **reads as
AI-generated SEO content farms for inventory-software affiliates**, producing confident,
precisely-numbered benchmarks with no attribution. **Do not stake a pivot on any number appearing
only in that cluster.**

**Genuinely independent sources here:** Ohio State News, Parsa (academic), Nation's Restaurant News,
Restaurant Business, IBISWorld, USDA, Wolf Street, Bankrate, Paycor, Forbes/Georgetown, and the
Independent Restaurant Coalition (biased *against* delivery apps specifically).
