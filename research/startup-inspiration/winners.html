The competition on your own campus

UCSB New Venture Competition

Running since 1999, now 27 years old. Not a weekend hackathon — an eight-month program that walks you from idea to pitch, open to students of every major and every level. More than 70 startups have launched out of it, raising over $1.2 billion between them. ⚠️

The program runs on the academic calendar. Information sessions are held each October.
QuarterWhat happens
FallIdea development, pitch events, student mixers and team formation. Info sessions in October.
WinterBusiness-model workshops. Introductions to industry mentors.
SpringLearn to tell the story of the business. Prep for the Finals in May.
Timing

It is August 2026. The October info sessions are about six weeks out, and fall quarter is when teams form. You would arrive with a product that already runs — which is more than almost anyone at those mixers has.

The case study to study

The 2026 winner

Won · 27th NVC Finals, 20 May 2026 Prize · $10,000 Team · 2

LegalTime AI

Joaquin Del Pego (Computer Science) & Quinn Godfrensen (Mathematics)

Automated billable-hour capture for law firms. Hands-free, processed on-device for client privacy, with native Clio integration. They pitched a 10× return on investment for the firm.

Both founders arrived with experience across four tech startups — machine learning, sales automation, AI enablement, backend. Credibility was established before the competition, not during it.

The honest asterisk: they aren't alone. Billables AI is an existing venture-backed startup doing automated legal time-tracking, covered by trade press in December 2025. Even a competition winner judged by VCs is in a contested space. Contested is normal. Not knowing you're contested is the problem.

Steal this

They interviewed more than eighty law firms, and the customer research was the pitch. Not the demo. Not the model. The eighty conversations. Your fifteen restaurant interviews are the same instrument — and they're worth more to a judge than a working backend.

The alumni that made it worth entering

  • Inogen — NVC winner, founded 2001 by three UCSB students. Portable oxygen concentrators. Went public.
  • Apeel Sciences — NVC 2012 winner. A plant-derived coating that extends produce shelf life. Raised $640M from backers including Temasek; valued above $2 billion in 2021 with 450 employees.
  • Salty Girl Seafood (2014), AppScale, MILO, EVmatch.
Cautionary · the same company

Apeel today: roughly 50 staff, down from 450

The company spent the better part of three years fighting a coordinated online misinformation campaign about its coating, and never got back to the 2021 valuation. No competitor beat it.

Steal this

The closest thing to your problem that UCSB ever produced — food spoilage — became a unicorn and then lost 90% of its staff to public perception. Anything that touches what people put in their mouths carries a risk that has nothing to do with whether the product works.

Read before pitching anything restaurant-shaped

Already in your lane

Two YC-funded companies are building what the restaurant pivot would be. Both are worth studying precisely because of how they solved the problems the restaurant research flagged.

YC · S26Founded · 2026New York · 2 employees

Marble

"The first AI-native operating system for the restaurant back-of-house"

  • Computer vision and voice AI capture real-time inventory ~90% faster than manual counts
  • ML demand forecasting claimed at 97% item-level accuracy
  • Agents run procurement, invoice reconciliation, scheduling, food prep and menu engineering
  • Three national chain contracts in progress; $3M in sales processed ⚠️ company-stated

Founders: Aakar Khanna (CEO) — Goldman Sachs investment banking covering restaurants and F&B, then early BizOps at AtoB (YC S20). Arjun Chaliha (CTO) — Senior Software Engineer at Bloomberg, leading AI/ML for electronic trading.

Steal this

Two independent confirmations of the restaurant research, from people with money on the line. They went straight to computer vision and voice because you cannot make a human log things during service — the exact conclusion the waste research reached. And they sell to national chains, not independents — the exact conclusion the vendor landscape reached.

YC · F25Founded · May 2025Seed · $500K, Sept 2025

Zavo

Agentic point-of-sale for restaurants and retail — Can Zehebi & Ilkan Gezer

Payments, POS and AI agents in one platform. Started by talking to small-business owners frustrated by fragmented systems. 400+ businesses live, from coffee shops to full-service restaurants.

Steal this

They didn't build a module for the POS. They built the POS. That's the other way past the distribution tax — own the register instead of renting space on someone else's. It is a much harder company to build, and it's the only structural answer to the 30%-in-perpetuity problem.

Also worth a look in YC's food stack: BetterBasket (AI pricing, competitive intelligence and promotion agents for grocery retailers) and GrubMarket (AI-powered digital transformation of the American food supply chain).

The extracted pattern

What actually wins

Ranked by how consistently it showed up across every winner on this page.

1

Customer interviews are the spine of the pitch

Eighty law firms. Zavo started by talking to small-business owners. This isn't preparation for the pitch — it is the pitch. It's also the only part you can do this month, for about $60.

2

A narrow, unglamorous vertical

Legal timekeeping. Collectible-dealer listings. Hospital waste sorting. Nobody won with "AI for X" where X was large. CoListable made the finals selling to people who resell watches.

3

Founder-problem fit, established beforehand

Apeel came out of materials research. Marble's CEO covered restaurants at Goldman. Recipe Cost Calculator's founder owned a bakery for a decade. Winners have a reason to be the ones doing it — and a semester spent in a kitchen is a cheap way to acquire one.

4

A business or institutional buyer

Every recoverable 2026 finalist. Every student food-waste winner. Consumer apps do not win these competitions, and the CAC math in the earlier research says they don't win outside them either.

5

Two founders, complementary

Never solo, in any case found. Usually one who builds and one who sells.

6

One number the judge remembers

LegalTime: 10× ROI. Sustainabite: 25–30% reduction. Marble: 90% faster counts, 97% forecast accuracy. Pick the number before you build the deck.

Your next eight months

Mapped onto the competition calendar, starting from where you actually are today.

Now — Sept

Run the fifteen interviews

The questionnaire is written and the kill criteria are set. LegalTime did eighty; fifteen is your minimum viable version. Walk in at 2:30pm on a Tuesday. Cost: about $60 and one week.

October

Go to a New Venture Program info session

Free, open to any major, any level. Enroll on the program's Canvas page so you get the updates.

Fall quarter

Find your second founder at the mixers

Statistics & Data Science covers the technical half. You need the half who will walk into fifteen restaurants with you. Nobody on this page won alone.

Winter quarter

Business model and mentors

This is where the interview data turns into a model — and where a mentor tells you, for free, what the platform tax and the $99 Square floor mean for your pricing.

Spring · May

Finals

$75,000 pool. Six finalist slots. Pitch the business, not the app.

One more thing
