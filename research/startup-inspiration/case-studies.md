## THE 2026 WINNER — LegalTime AI

**Joaquin Del Pego (CS) + Quinn Godfrensen (Math).** Won the 27th NVC Finals, 20 May 2026. $10,000.

**The product:** automated billable-hour capture for law firms. Hands-free, on-device for privacy,
native Clio integration. Claimed **10× ROI** for the firm.

**Why they won — and this is the part to copy:**

> ⭐ **They interviewed more than eighty law firms** before the pitch, and the customer research *was*
> the pitch. Not the demo, not the model architecture — the eighty conversations.

Both founders had prior experience across four tech startups (ML, sales automation, AI enablement,
backend). **Founder-problem credibility was established before the competition, not during it.**

**The honest asterisk:** they are not alone. **Billables AI** is an existing venture-backed startup
doing automated legal time-tracking, covered by Legal IT Insider in December 2025. Even the winner of
a competition judged by VCs is in a contested space. *Contested is normal. Contested is not
disqualifying. Not knowing you're contested is.*

## THE ALUMNI — the reason the competition is worth entering

- **Inogen** — NVC winner, founded 2001 by three UCSB students. Portable oxygen concentrators.
  **Went public.**
- **Apeel Sciences** — **NVC 2012 winner.** Plant-derived coating that extends produce shelf life.
  Raised **$640M** from backers including Temasek; **valued above $2B** in 2021 with 450 employees.
  **This is the closest thing to PantryChef's problem that has ever come out of your school — food
  spoilage — and it became a unicorn.**
- **Salty Girl Seafood** (2014), AppScale, MILO, EVmatch.

### ⚠️ And the cautionary half of that same story

**Apeel today has roughly 50 staff, down from 450.** The company spent the best part of three years
fighting a coordinated online misinformation campaign about its coating, and never recovered the
2021 valuation.

> **The best food-spoilage outcome in UCSB history is also the best warning: a consumer-facing food
> product can be killed by public perception faster than by any competitor.** Worth remembering for
> anything that touches what people eat.

## THE STARTUPS ALREADY IN YOUR LANE — read before pitching anything restaurant-shaped

### Marble (YC S26) — the direct one

**"The first AI-native operating system for the restaurant back-of-house."** Founded 2026, New York,
two employees.

- **Computer vision and voice AI capture real-time inventory ~90% faster than manual counts**
- ML demand forecasting claimed at **97% item-level accuracy**
- AI agents run procurement, invoice reconciliation, scheduling, food prep and menu engineering
- **Three national chain contracts in progress; $3M in sales processed** ⚠️ *company-stated*

**Founders:** Aakar Khanna (CEO) — Goldman Sachs investment banking **covering restaurants and F&B**,
then early BizOps at AtoB (YC S20), then Head of Strategy & Ops at Kouper. Arjun Chaliha (CTO) —
Senior Software Engineer at Bloomberg leading AI/ML for electronic trading.

> **Two things to take from Marble.** First: they went straight to **computer vision and voice** to
> capture inventory — independently arriving at the same conclusion the waste research reached, that
> you cannot make a human log things during service. Second: they sell to **national chains**, not
> independents — again matching what Winnow and Leanpath learned.

### Zavo (YC F25) — the flank

Agentic point-of-sale for restaurants and retail. Can Zehebi + Ilkan Gezer, founded May 2025 after
talking to small-business owners frustrated by fragmented systems. **400+ businesses live. $500K seed,
September 2025.**

**They didn't build a module for the POS. They built the POS.** That's the other way through the
distribution problem — and it is much harder.

### Also in the YC food stack

**GrubMarket** — AI-powered digital transformation of the American food supply chain.

## WHAT ACTUALLY WINS — the extracted pattern

**1. A narrow, unglamorous vertical.** Legal timekeeping. Collectible-dealer listings. Hospital waste
sorting. Nobody won with "AI for X" where X was large. **CoListable made the finals selling to people
who resell watches.**

**2. Customer interviews are the spine of the pitch.** Eighty law firms. Zavo started by talking to
small-business owners. This is not preparation for the pitch — **it is the pitch.** Your fifteen
restaurant interviews are worth more to a judge than the working backend.

**3. Founder-problem fit, established beforehand.** Apeel came out of materials research. Marble's CEO
covered restaurants at Goldman. Recipe Cost Calculator's founder owned a bakery for a decade. **The
teams that win have a reason to be the ones doing it.**

**4. One number.** LegalTime: 10× ROI. Sustainabite: 25–30% reduction. Marble: 90% faster counts, 97%
forecast accuracy. Judges remember a number.

**5. Two founders, complementary.** Never solo. Usually one who builds and one who sells.

**6. Business or institutional buyer.** Every recoverable 2026 finalist. Consumer apps do not win
these.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU — concretely

1. **October: go to the New Venture Program info session.** Eight months, free, $75K pool, and it is
   explicitly open to undergraduates of any major. You have a working product — almost nobody at the
   fall mixers does.
2. **Fall quarter is team formation.** You need a second founder. Every winner had one. Statistics &
   Data Science is the technical half; find the half that will walk into fifteen restaurants with you.
3. **Run the fifteen interviews first, in September.** LegalTime did eighty. Fifteen is your minimum
   viable version of the thing that actually wins.
4. **Pitch the business, not the consumer app.** The recoverable finalist slate is 6/6 B2B or
   institutional. PantryChef-for-consumers does not fit the shape of what wins here.
5. **Vimicx exists.** The underwater-AR idea you liked was a 2026 finalist in your own competition.
   That's not a reason to drop it — it's evidence the judges reward that kind of ambition.
6. **Remember Apeel both ways.** The biggest food outcome UCSB ever produced, and a company that went
   from 450 people to 50 without a competitor beating it.
